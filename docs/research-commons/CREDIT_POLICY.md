# Credit and stewardship

Credit in this repository follows the artifact. An accepted receipt names the
person who returned the work and the exact public paths or evidence for which
credit is being recorded. It does not reassign authorship of the surrounding
corpus, and it does not imply that every idea mentioned during an exploration
became part of the accepted result.

Different roles remain different. A contributor may be distinct from a
collaborator, from the person operating a tool, and from a disclosed model or
service used during the work. The receipt keeps those identities separate so
that assistance is visible without obscuring human responsibility or inventing
authorship. Undisclosed information stays undisclosed rather than being
guessed.

Positive, negative, inconclusive, and corrective work can all receive credit.
The relevant question is whether the returned artifact is accepted as useful,
bounded, and traceable—not whether it looks like a theorem or increases a
score. The repository does not rank contributors by commits, lines changed,
receipt counts, model usage, or any other activity proxy.

Submission is not acceptance. Issues, pull requests, and Git history preserve
delivery provenance, but work enters the accepted-only contribution views only
after an accepted receipt is committed alongside an exact public commit. That
acceptance does not by itself establish correctness, novelty, peer review,
release inclusion, or a change in mathematical claim status; those facts have
their own evidence and authorities.

Corrections preserve lineage. A newer receipt names the earlier return and
records whether it retains, supersedes, or withdraws the affected artifact.
The earlier contributor and historical evidence remain discoverable. Disputes
about attribution should be handled as corrective contributions with concrete
artifact and provenance evidence rather than by rewriting history silently.

All contributions remain subject to the repository's licences and source
attribution requirements. A contributor should cite prior work, identify any
material collaborators, and state limitations honestly. The maintainer's
stewardship duty is to keep that credit narrow, durable, public, and no stronger
than the accepted evidence.
