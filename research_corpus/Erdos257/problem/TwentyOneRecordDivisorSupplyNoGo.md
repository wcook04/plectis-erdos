# Static divisor supply fails, but dyadic record inheritance survives

Status: exact finite counterexamples on the actual denominator-21 greedy
orbit, 2026-08-22.  These counterexamples do not decide the record-reset or
record-repair conjectures and do not decide Erdős Problem 257.

## 1. Motivation

The strict-record experiment in
[`TwentyOneComputationalStructureLab.md`](TwentyOneComputationalStructureLab.md)
observes that if the Lambert defect `Q_n` sets a new record, the selected
divisors of `n+1` immediately reset the next defect to at most four.  The
strong candidate `(RB4)` is

\[
 Q_{n+1}\leq 4,
\]

and the weaker `(RR)` candidate is

\[
 f(n+1)\geq Q_n+t_{n+1},
 \qquad f(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}b_d.
\]

The successor loads are close to half of the ordinary divisor count, so three
static explanations are tempting:

1. complementary divisors `d` and `(n+1)/d` contribute exactly one selected
   member;
2. at least half of all successor divisors are selected;
3. one fixed side of the square-root pairing supplies the load.

All three explanations are false on the exact orbit.

## 2. Exact counterexamples

The computation reconstructs the rational greedy word using integer dyadic
intervals at precision `2N+160`.  Branch decisions and divisor enumeration use
no floating point.

At the strict record `n=79`, the successor is `80`.  Among its proper
complementary divisor pairs,

\[
 (b_2,b_{40})=(0,0),\qquad (b_5,b_{16})=(1,1).
\]

Thus neither “at least one” nor “at most one” holds pairwise; in particular,
there is no exact one-per-pair involution behind the load.

Exact half-density already fails at `n=319`: the successor `320` has fourteen
divisors and eight selected divisors.  Even the lower bound “at least half are
selected” later fails at `n=1439`:

\[
 \tau(1440)=36,\qquad f(1440)=17.
\]

The load also cannot be assigned uniformly to one side of the square-root
pairing.  Starting with the mature records whose successors are divisible by
`60`, the successor `420` has four selected divisors at or below its square
root and eight above it.  At successor `1440`, the direction reverses: nine
are at or below the square root and eight are above it.

These are finite counterexamples to universal supply lemmas, rather than
statistical deviations.

## 3. A positive dyadic inheritance candidate

The same exact replay exposes a source-conditioned statement that survives
all tested parent records.  If `r` is a strict record, put

\[
 c=2r+1.
\]

For every such `r` with `c+1<=10080`, the computation verifies

\[
 \boxed{f(r+1)\geq Q_c+t_{c+1}.}                 \tag{DI}
\]

This is stronger than a correlation between two large loads: it says the old
load at the parent successor already pays the child's repair bill.  The
minimum checked margin in `(DI)` is zero.  Thirteen parent records are tested,
including the six parent-child record links

\[
419\to839\to1679\to3359\to6719
\]

and

\[
2519\to5039\to10079.
\]

If the child `c=2r+1` is itself a strict record, then

\[
 r+1\mid c+1=2r+2.
\]

Every selected divisor of `r+1` is therefore a selected divisor of `c+1`, so

\[
 f(c+1)\geq f(r+1)\geq Q_c+t_{c+1}.
\]

This is exactly `(RR)` at the child.  Thus an all-depth proof of `(DI)` would
remove every dyadically inherited record from the repair problem.  It would
leave only the root records, meaning strict records not of the form `2r+1`
for an earlier strict record `r`.

The exact equivalent form

\[
 Q_{2r+1}\leq
 2Q_r+t_{r+1}-Q_{r+1}-t_{2r+2}
\]

shows the remaining mathematics: a record-conditioned dyadic block bound,
not a static density theorem on the divisor lattice.

## 4. Analytic consequence

The observed near-half load is not generated by a static complement symmetry,
a universal half-density inequality, or a fixed lower/upper divisor bank.
Consequently none of those mechanisms can prove `(RB4)` or `(RR)`.

The surviving theorem must use the strict-record condition jointly with the
source-sensitive ancestry of the same greedy word.  In particular, an
argument may still compare `f(n+1)` with `Q_n`; it cannot replace that joint
comparison by a target-free statement about how Boolean selections populate
the divisor lattice of `n+1`.

The dyadic inequality `(DI)` supplies one concrete version of that joint
comparison and proves `(RR)` on the checked child-record class.  The next probe
should therefore separate root-record birth from dyadic propagation and test
whether `(DI)` has an all-depth block proof.  The counterexamples here do not
falsify `(RB4)` or `(RR)` themselves.
