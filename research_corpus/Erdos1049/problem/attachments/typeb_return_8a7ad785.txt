# Verdict

I have **not** obtained a complete proof that

[
S_{3,2}:=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{(3/2)^m-1}
]

is irrational. I did obtain three substantive results:

1. A rational homogenisation of Zudilin’s permutation-enhanced hypergeometric approximants that **strictly improves the R3 rational-base region** and proves an infinite new family of non-integral rational bases.
2. An exact optimisation theorem showing that the standard rectangular two-function Hermite–Padé construction cannot improve the R3 threshold at all.
3. An exact (2)-adic/(3)-adic calculation at (3/2) showing why the promising large (3)-adic factor in the hypergeometric coefficient does **not** survive in the rationality-forced integer linear form.

Thus the tested simultaneous and adelic constructions do not yet prove the (3/2) case, but the result is considerably sharper than another unsuccessful parameter search: it gives a new irrationality theorem, an infinite family beyond R3, and a quantitative specification of the local gain that a successful (3/2) construction must supply. This addresses the packet’s required fallback rather than treating the absence of a known proof as an impossibility argument. 

The distinction matters because R3 only ruled out the literal coordinatewise corridor; it did not rule out simultaneous cancellation or a genuine product-formula construction.

# 1. A stronger rational-base theorem

Write

[
h_p(1):=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{p^m-1},
\qquad p=\frac ab>1,\qquad (a,b)=1.
]

I use the hypergeometric construction underlying Zudilin’s best published irrationality-measure estimate for (h_p(1)) when (p) is an integer. The source provides the partial fractions, cyclotomic divisor, permutation gain, positivity, and asymptotic constants; the rational-base homogeneous evaluation below is the additional step. ([[Polish Mathematical Institute](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/82435)][1])

## 1.1 Explicit approximants

Put (q=p^{-1}), and for (n\ge1) take

[
a_0=14n+1,\qquad
a_1=12n+1,\qquad
a_2=14n+1,\qquad
B=27n+2.
]

For ((x;q)*r=\prod*{j=0}^{r-1}(1-xq^j)), define

[
R_n(T)=
\frac{(qT;q)*{a_1-1}}{(q;q)*{a_1-1}},
\frac{(q;q)*{B-a_2-1}}
{(q^{a_2}T;q)*{B-a_2-1}},
T^{a_0}.
]

For (p>1), every factor of (R_n(q^t)) is positive. Hence

[
F_n(p):=\sum_{t\ge0}R_n(q^t)>0.
]

The partial-fraction expansion is

[
R_n(T)=T^{a_0}\sum_{k=a_2}^{B-1}
\frac{A_{n,k}}{1-q^kT},
]

where

[
A_{n,k}
=(-1)^{a_1+a_2+k+1}p^{E_{n,k}}
{k-1\brack a_1-1}_p
{B-a_2-1\brack B-k-1}_p
]

and

[
E_{n,k}
=======

\frac{a_1(a_1-1)}2
-\frac{(B-a_2)(B-a_2-1)}2
+\frac{(B-k)(B-k-1)}2.
]

Consequently,

[
F_n(p)=A_n(p)h_p(1)-B_{1,n}(p)-B_{2,n}(p),
]

with

[
A_n(p)=\sum_{k=a_2}^{B-1}A_{n,k}p^{a_0k},
]

[
B_{1,n}(p)=
\sum_{k=a_2}^{B-1}A_{n,k}p^{a_0k}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-a_1}\frac1{p^\ell-1},
]

and

[
B_{2,n}(p)=
\sum_{k=a_2}^{B-1}\sum_{j=1}^{a_0-1}
\frac{A_{n,k}p^{a_0k-j(k-a_1)}}{p^j-1}.
]

The exponent in the last display is (a_0k-j(k-a_1)); this is the version for which the exact identity with the positive sum (F_n) holds.

## 1.2 Cyclotomic denominator and polynomial degrees

Set

[
M_n=266n^2+34n+1
]

and

[
D_{15n}(p)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{15n}\Phi_\ell(p).
]

Define

[
\nu_\ell=\max\left{
\begin{aligned}
&0,\
&\left\lfloor\frac{14n}{\ell}\right\rfloor+
\left\lfloor\frac{13n}{\ell}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{12n}{\ell}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{15n}{\ell}\right\rfloor,\
&2\left\lfloor\frac{14n}{\ell}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{13n}{\ell}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{15n}{\ell}\right\rfloor
\end{aligned}
\right}
]

and

[
\Omega_n(p)=\prod_{\ell=2}^{15n}\Phi_\ell(p)^{\nu_\ell}.
]

For this parameter direction the source proves that the (\nu_\ell) are (0) or (1), and that

[
\Lambda_n(p):=
p^{-M_n}\frac{D_{15n}(p)}{\Omega_n(p)}F_n(p)
=U_n(p)h_p(1)-V_n(p)
]

has

[
U_n,V_n\in\mathbb Z[p].
]

The degree calculation is finite and especially clean. The degree of the (k)-th summand of (A_n) is

[
d_{n,k}
=a_0k+E_{n,k}
+(a_1-1)(k-a_1)
+(B-k-1)(k-a_2),
]

and direct subtraction gives

[
\boxed{d_{n,k+1}-d_{n,k}=40n+1-k.}
]

Since (k\le B-2=27n), these differences are positive. Hence the last summand controls the degree:

[
\boxed{
K_n:=\deg A_n
=\frac{1091n^2+81n+2}{2}.}
]

Now put

[
W_n=
K_n-M_n+
\sum_{\ell=1}^{15n}\varphi(\ell)
-\sum_{\ell=2}^{15n}\nu_\ell\varphi(\ell).
]

Then

[
\deg U_n=W_n,\qquad \deg V_n=W_n-1,
]

and their leading coefficients have absolute value (1).

## 1.3 Rational homogenisation

For reduced (a>b\ge1), define

[
\widehat U_n(a,b)=b^{W_n}U_n(a/b),\qquad
\widehat V_n(a,b)=b^{W_n}V_n(a/b).
]

Because the polynomial degrees are at most (W_n),

[
\widehat U_n(a,b),\widehat V_n(a,b)\in\mathbb Z.
]

Moreover,

[
\widehat\Lambda_n(a,b)
:=\widehat U_n(a,b)S_{a,b}-\widehat V_n(a,b)
=b^{W_n}\Lambda_n(a/b)>0.
]

Positivity gives exact nonvanishing; no determinant estimate is required here.

The published asymptotic constants for this direction are

[
C_1=\frac{1091}{2}=545.5,
\qquad
C_0=221.30008816\ldots,
]

with

[
\frac{W_n}{n^2}\longrightarrow C_1-C_0
]

and

[
\frac1{n^2}\log \Lambda_n(a/b)
\longrightarrow
-C_0\log(a/b).
]

It follows that

[
\boxed{
\frac1{n^2}\log\widehat\Lambda_n(a,b)
\longrightarrow
C_1\log b-C_0\log a.}
]

Assume now that (S_{a,b}=P/Q) in lowest terms. Then

[
Q\widehat\Lambda_n(a,b)
=P\widehat U_n(a,b)-Q\widehat V_n(a,b)
]

is a positive integer. It cannot tend to zero. Therefore:

[
\boxed{
\frac{\log b}{\log a}<\frac{C_0}{C_1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S_{a,b}\notin\mathbb Q.}
]

Numerically,

[
\frac{C_0}{C_1}
=0.40568302138\ldots,
]

whereas the R3/Bundschuh–Väänänen boundary is

[
\frac12-\frac1{\pi^2}
=0.39867881635\ldots.
]

Thus this is a strict enlargement of the rational-base region.

## 1.4 An exact rational lower bound for the new constant

The source’s permutation calculation expresses

[
C_0=266-\frac3{\pi^2}(225-J),
]

where (J) is a sum of trigamma differences over thirteen rational intervals. For each (0<u<v),

[
\psi_1(u)-\psi_1(v)
===================

\sum_{k\ge0}
\frac{(v-u)(2k+u+v)}
{(k+u)^2(k+v)^2}.
]

Every summand is positive. Retaining only (0\le k<10) in the thirteen source intervals gives, by exact rational arithmetic,

[
J>\frac{389699}{5000}=77.9398.
]

Using (\pi>157/50),

[
C_0>
266-\frac{3}{(157/50)^2}
\left(225-\frac{389699}{5000}\right)

>

\frac{88371}{400}.
]

But

[
\frac{88371}{400}
=\frac{81}{200},\frac{1091}{2}
=\frac{81}{200}C_1.
]

Hence the convenient exact bound

[
\boxed{\frac{C_0}{C_1}>\frac{81}{200}.}
]

# 2. A genuinely new infinite family beyond R3

Consider

[
a=31^r,\qquad b=4^r,\qquad r\ge1.
]

The logarithmic ratio is independent of (r):

[
\frac{\log b}{\log a}
=\frac{\log4}{\log31}.
]

We have the exact integer inequalities

[
2^{84}<31^{17},\qquad 2^{64}<31^{13}.
]

Therefore

[
4^{200}=2^{400}
=2^{4\cdot84+64}
<31^{4\cdot17+13}
=31^{81},
]

and so

[
\frac{\log4}{\log31}<\frac{81}{200}<\frac{C_0}{C_1}.
]

It follows that

[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac1{\bigl((31/4)^r\bigr)^m-1}
\text{ is irrational for every }r\ge1.}
]

This family is not covered by R3. Indeed,

[
4^5=1024>961=31^2,
]

so

[
\frac{\log4}{\log31}>\frac25.
]

Also (\pi^2<10), for example from (\pi<22/7), and therefore

[
\frac12-\frac1{\pi^2}<\frac25.
]

Thus

[
\frac{\log4}{\log31}>
\frac25>
\frac12-\frac1{\pi^2}.
]

The theorem above therefore crosses the previous one-place boundary on an explicit infinite set, as required by the fallback clause of the attack brief. 

The hypergeometric engine itself is published for integral (p); the homogeneous rational-base theorem and the (31/4) family are derivations from it. I have not established a priority claim for this rational specialisation, in accordance with the packet’s prior-art rules.

# 3. Exact (2)-adic and (3)-adic budget at (p=3/2)

The preceding construction has a surprisingly large (3)-adic factor in one coefficient. It is nevertheless the wrong kind of factor.

Put

[
\widehat U_n:=2^{W_n}U_n(3/2),
\qquad
\widehat V_n:=2^{W_n}V_n(3/2).
]

## 3.1 Order of (U_n) at (p=0)

For the general hypergeometric parameters, the minimum (p)-degree in the raw (A)-sum occurs uniquely at (k=a_2). After the normalisation by (p^{-M_n}), its order is

[
(a_2-a_1)(a_0+a_2-B).
]

For the selected direction,

[
a_2-a_1=2n,\qquad
a_0+a_2-B=n,
]

and hence

[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_{p=0}U_n=2n^2.}
]

More precisely,

[
U_n(p)=p^{2n^2}\widetilde U_n(p),
\qquad
\widetilde U_n(0)=\pm1.
]

Homogeneous evaluation at (p=3/2) therefore gives

[
\boxed{v_3(\widehat U_n)=2n^2.}
]

## 3.2 Constant coefficient of (V_n)

Expanding the normalised (B_1+B_2) expression at (p=0), the (B_1) part has positive order, while the transformed (B_2) sum has one surviving constant term. Including the constant term of (D_{15n}/\Omega_n) gives

[
\boxed{V_n(0)=1.}
]

Consequently,

[
\boxed{v_3(\widehat V_n)=0.}
]

At the other local place, the leading terms give

[
\boxed{v_2(\widehat U_n)=0,\qquad
v_2(\widehat V_n)=1.}
]

The first three exact finite regressions are

[
W_1=333,\qquad W_2=1315,\qquad W_3=2944,
]

and

[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
n&1&2&3\ \hline
v_3(\widehat U_n)&2&8&18\
v_3(\widehat V_n)&0&0&0\
v_2(\widehat U_n)&0&0&0\
v_2(\widehat V_n)&1&1&1
\end{array}
]

These are checks of the general formulae, not evidence for a new asymptotic assertion.

## 3.3 Why the large (3)-adic factor disappears

Suppose

[
S_{3,2}=\frac PQ,\qquad (P,Q)=1,
]

and form the integer

[
X_n=P\widehat U_n-Q\widehat V_n
=Q\widehat\Lambda_n>0.
]

For sufficiently large (n),

[
v_3(P\widehat U_n)
=v_3(P)+2n^2

> v_3(Q)
> =v_3(Q\widehat V_n).
> ]

The two summands have unequal (3)-adic valuations. Therefore the ultrametric equality gives

[
\boxed{v_3(X_n)=v_3(Q)}
]

for every sufficiently large (n).

Thus the quadratic factor (3^{2n^2}) in (\widehat U_n) is **one-sided**. It does not divide the rationality-forced integer linear form. At (2), the coefficient pair has no common positive valuation at all:

[
\min{v_2(\widehat U_n),v_2(\widehat V_n)}=0.
]

This is exactly the denominator-transfer phenomenon represented abstractly in the supplied adelic-height file: local content in one displayed coefficient is not a gain unless it survives in the common integer obstruction. The supplied Lean modules contain finite product-formula and determinant interfaces, not an irrationality theorem for this value. 

## 3.4 The complete exponent deficit at (3/2)

The homogeneous archimedean exponent is

[
E_{3/2}
=C_1\log2-C_0\log3
=134.98879065\ldots>0.
]

Hence these homogeneous forms do not tend to zero at (3/2).

Even counterfactually treating the entire factor (3^{2n^2}) in (\widehat U_n) as a common divisor would only change the exponent to

[
E_{3/2}-2\log3
=132.791566\ldots>0.
]

A successful descendant of this construction therefore needs, in natural-log units, a common local gain

[
\boxed{G>134.98879065\ldots}
]

per (n^2). Equivalently, it needs at least one of

[
v_3(X_n)>
\left(\frac{E_{3/2}}{\log3}+o(1)\right)n^2
==========================================

(122.87\ldots+o(1))n^2,
]

or

[
v_2(X_n)>
\left(\frac{E_{3/2}}{\log2}+o(1)\right)n^2
==========================================

(194.75\ldots+o(1))n^2,
]

or a mixed (2)-/(3)-adic gain of the corresponding weighted size.

The actual (3)-adic valuation is eventually the fixed number (v_3(Q)).

# 4. The rectangular simultaneous Hermite–Padé family is optimally no better than R3

Postelmans and Van Assche construct simultaneous approximants to the first two (q)-zeta values, proving linear independence when (q^{-1}) is an integer. Their construction is a natural candidate for a rational-base simultaneous attack, but the exact homogeneous budget shows that its additional vanishing is precisely cancelled by its additional polynomial width. ([[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0604312)][2])

Let the two approximation degrees have ratio

[
\frac mn\longrightarrow\rho,\qquad 0\le\rho\le1,
]

and let the evaluation point satisfy

[
\frac Ln\longrightarrow\sigma,\qquad
\sigma\ge1+\rho.
]

The exact quadratic exponents are:

[
c(\rho,\sigma)
=\frac{1+\rho^2}{2}+\sigma
]

for the raw archimedean decay,

[
h(\rho,\sigma)
=\frac{(1+\rho)^2}{2}+\sigma(1+\rho)
]

for homogeneous polynomial width, and

[
\delta(\sigma)=\frac{3\sigma^2}{\pi^2}
]

for the cyclotomic saving.

The resulting rational-base boundary is

[
\Theta(\rho,\sigma)
===================

\frac{c(\rho,\sigma)-\delta(\sigma)}
{h(\rho,\sigma)+c(\rho,\sigma)}.
]

I obtain the exact optimisation

[
\boxed{
\Theta(\rho,\sigma)
\le
\frac12-\frac1{\pi^2},}
]

with equality only at

[
\rho=0,\qquad \sigma=1,
]

which is the ordinary one-function Padé endpoint.

Indeed, write

[
\sigma=1+\rho+u,\qquad u\ge0.
]

After multiplying by the positive denominator, the desired inequality is equivalent to

[
(\pi^2+2)c-6\sigma^2-(\pi^2-2)h\le0.
]

Direct expansion gives

[
\begin{aligned}
&(\pi^2+2)c-6\sigma^2-(\pi^2-2)h\
={}&-\pi^2\rho^2-\pi^2\rho u-2\pi^2\rho
-2\rho^2-10\rho u-4\rho-6u^2-8u,
\end{aligned}
]

which is non-positive and vanishes only when (\rho=u=0).

Therefore this entire rectangular two-function family cannot even strictly improve R3, let alone reach

[
\frac{\log2}{\log3}
=0.63092975\ldots.
]

This is a family-level optimality theorem, not merely a failed choice of (m,n,L).

A related general obstruction is worth recording. Any single polynomial-form construction with coefficient exponent (C_1-C_0), error exponent (C_0), and no common specialised local divisor has rational-base boundary (C_0/C_1). Since its integer-base irrationality exponent is (C_1/C_0\ge2), that boundary is at most (1/2). But

[
\frac{\log2}{\log3}>\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
4>3.
]

Thus **no one-form homogeneous construction without a genuine common local gain can reach (3/2)**.

# 5. Why merely comparing the real and (2)-adic Lambert sums is insufficient

The series

[
\sum_{m\ge1}\tau(m)(2/3)^m
]

converges both in (\mathbb R) and in (\mathbb Q_2). Those two limits need not be the same rational number. Consequently, assuming that the real limit is (P/Q) does not identify the (2)-adic limit with (P/Q).

A valid adelic proof must therefore construct a **single rational or integral object**

[
X_n=P A_n-QB_n
]

whose archimedean absolute value and local valuations are simultaneously controlled. Separate convergence statements in different completions do not enter the product formula for the same number.

For a nonzero integer (X),

[
\log |X|
\ge v_2(X)\log2+v_3(X)\log3.
]

The remaining task is precisely to build nonzero (X_n) for which the reverse strict inequality follows from the approximation estimates.

**Established in this response**

[

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

[

is irrational for every (r\ge1), an infinite family lying strictly outside the R3 region.

Also established:

* the rectangular two-function Hermite–Padé threshold is optimally no better than the one-function threshold;
* the Zudilin coefficient pair at (3/2) has exact local valuations
  [
  (v_3(\widehat U_n),v_3(\widehat V_n))=(2n^2,0),

**Not established**

[
