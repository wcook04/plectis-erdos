### `erdos1049.prime_support_selectors`

**sharp prime-supported two-selector rational gap, rational no-double-decay, one-row prime gaps, determinant-height tradeoff, and reduced selector collision**

For an integral linear form B (a/q) - A at a rational target with q > 0, a single prime l dividing B and dividing neither A nor q forces the sharp gap |B (a/q) - A| ≥ 1/q, and the same gap follows from l^r | B for any exponent r ≠ 0. For a pair of rows the numerator hypothesis can be dropped: if l divides both B_1 and B_2 and l^2 does not divide the exterior determinant A_1 B_2 - A_2 B_1, then at least one of the two rows already has the full 1/q gap. Along any sequence of integral row pairs whose exterior determinant is nonzero at every index, the two real linear forms cannot both tend to zero at a rational target, so no such pair of selectors can jointly witness vanishing. Recombination does not evade this: a real change of basis whose determinant has absolute value 1 and whose four entries are bounded by H, making both recombined remainders at most epsilon, forces |A_1 B_2 - A_2 B_1| ≤ 2 H epsilon (|B_1| + |B_2|), so joint decay is paid for in determinant size, and integer-unimodular recombination is the special case. When every denominator coordinate vanishes modulo N the pigeonhole only has to see the numerator coordinate, so N < 2^k already forces a nontrivial binary selector collision in both coordinates. For a q-Apéry approach to the Lambert value at 3/2 the consequence is a precise specification of what is still missing: prime or prime-power support in the denominator coordinates that persists cofinally, together with determinant control that survives recombination; the ancestry is the classical Apéry-style linear-form argument, and priority is unassessed. Producing that cofinal support for the actual q-Apéry rows, the analytic decay, and irrationality at 3/2 remain open.
