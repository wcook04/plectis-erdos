# Affine-defect rigidity near the factorial endpoint

Let

\[
e_n=n!-1-b_n,\qquad
\eta_n=b_{n+1}-(n+1)b_n-n
\]

for an integer sequence \(b_n\).  The exact identity

\[
\eta_n=(n+1)e_n-e_{n+1}
\]

turns a small affine defect into rapid growth of a positive downward error.
The Lean module `AffineDefectRigidity.lean` kernel-checks the following finite
and cofinal consequences under the one-sided downward-window hypotheses.

1. If \(e_n>0\) and \(\eta_k<k\) for all \(n\le k\le n+3\), then
   \(e_{n+4}>(n+4)^2+2\). Thus this defect block contradicts the
   quadratic upper bound at \(n+4\).
2. Consequently a nonzero downward perturbation in the eventual window
   \(0\le e_n\le n^2+2\) forces a spike \(\eta_k\ge k\) in its next four
   positions.
3. If the perturbation is cofinally nonzero while remaining in that window,
   those spikes occur cofinally.
4. Equivalently, an eventually sublinear defect \(\eta_n<n\) forces
   \(b_n=n!-1\) eventually.

The constant four is deliberately finite and uniform.  No density or
equidistribution input is used.

## Exact role in Erdős #68

This theorem is structurally independent of the factorial-digit and
prime-pole routes.  It says that any useful family of integer approximants
lying just below \(n!-1\) cannot be both nontrivial and affine-smooth.  What it
does **not** yet prove is that the weighted reciprocal transform generated by
the spikes is nonintegral or small enough to contradict a hypothetical
rational value of

\[
\sum_{n\ge2}\frac1{n!-1}.
\]

That weighted-transform bridge is the remaining target-facing obligation for
this lane; the rigidity theorem itself is exact and unconditional.
