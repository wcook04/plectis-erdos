## Occupied derivation A: componentwise radial-contour connector

Let `t>0` be a regular value of `|P|`, and let `C` be a component of
`{|P|<t}` containing exactly `k>=2` simple roots.  Put

```text
p_C(s) = H^1(C cap {|P|=s})
```

for almost every `0<s<=t`.  Then two roots in `C` are joined inside
`closure(C)` by a rectifiable path of length at most

```text
(1/k) [ p_C(t) + (1/pi) integral_0^t p_C(s) ds/s ].      (RC1)
```

To prove this, choose an argument `theta` avoided by all critical values in
`C`.  The `k` inverse lifts of `[0,t exp(i theta)]` join the `k` roots to
cyclically ordered boundary points.  Join each adjacent pair of lifts by the
intervening boundary arc.  If the lift lengths are `ell_j` and the boundary
arcs are `sigma_j`, the sum of the `k` resulting root-pair path lengths is

```text
2 sum_j ell_j + p_C(t).                                  (RC2)
```

The area formula and then coarea for `|P|` give

```text
integral_0^(2pi) sum_j ell_j(theta) dtheta
  = integral_C |P'(z)|/|P(z)| dA(z)
  = integral_0^t p_C(s) ds/s.                            (RC3)
```

Averaging `(RC2)` first over the adjacent pair and then over `theta` proves
`(RC1)`.  Consequently the component closes the normalized theorem whenever

```text
p_C(t) + (1/pi) integral_0^t p_C(s) ds/s <= 2k.          (RC4)
```

The proof is exact and attachment-compatible: the same component supplies
both radial lifts and the boundary connection.  The universal existence of a
component and level satisfying `(RC4)` is not proved.  This derivation is not
new: `UnconditionalConstantFactorBound.md` §§6–7 already owns equations `(C)`
and the same cyclic adjacent-lift plus boundary-arc average, then spends stronger
low/high-level estimates to obtain its unconditional constant-factor theorem.
`(RC4)` is a sharpened way to state the still-open constant-two consumer.

### Exact no-go: `(RC4)` is not a universal producer

Take `P(z)=z^2-r^2` with `0<r<1`.  A sublevel component contains both roots
only when `t>=r^2`.  Its regular boundary is a Jordan curve enclosing `-r` and
`r`, so

```text
p_C(t) >= 4r.                                           (RN1)
```

For every `0<s<r^2`, the lower level has two one-root Jordan components.  Each
has on its boundary the two real points `sqrt(r^2-s),sqrt(r^2+s)`, or their
negatives.  The two arcs of a Jordan curve between two boundary points each
have length at least their Euclidean distance.  Summing the two components,

```text
p_C(s) >= 4 [sqrt(r^2+s)-sqrt(r^2-s)].                  (RN2)
```

Every `t>=r^2` consequently satisfies

```text
p_C(t) + (1/pi) integral_0^t p_C(s) ds/s
 >= 4r + (4r/pi) J,

J = integral_0^1 [sqrt(1+u)-sqrt(1-u)] du/u
  = 2sqrt(2)-2log(1+sqrt(2)).                           (RN3)
```

Put `r_0=1/(1+J/pi)=0.7467052765...`.  For `r>r_0`, the right
side of `(RN3)` is strictly larger than `4=2k`.  Thus no multi-root level of
this open-unit-disk quadratic satisfies `(RC4)`, although the straight root
segment proves Erdős 1041 exactly.  A radial-contour packet must prove a
dichotomy or compensation theorem; it must not ask for universal `(RC4)`.

There is also a completely elementary rational witness.  Rationalizing the
integrand defining `J` gives

```text
[sqrt(1+u)-sqrt(1-u)]/u
  = 2/[sqrt(1+u)+sqrt(1-u)] >= 1/sqrt(2).
```

Hence `J>=1/sqrt(2)`.  Using `pi<22/7`,

```text
J/pi > 7/(22sqrt(2)) > 1/5,
```

where the last inequality squares to `1225>968`.  At the rational radius
`r=5/6`, `(RN3)` is therefore strictly larger than
`4r(1+1/5)=4`.  This is an exact witness without numerical evaluation of the
logarithm or threshold.

## Occupied derivation B: a concyclic starlike coordinate

Let `w_1,...,w_n` lie on the unit circle and
`g(z)=prod_j(z-w_j)`.  Choose the zero-free branch of `g^(2/n)` on the open
unit disk and define

```text
Phi(z) = z/g(z)^(2/n).
```

Then

```text
z Phi'(z)/Phi(z)
 = (1/n) sum_j (1+conj(w_j)z)/(1-conj(w_j)z),            (SC1)
```

whose real part is positive.  The standard starlikeness criterion therefore
makes `Phi` univalent with starlike image.  Moreover

```text
|g(z)| <= 1  iff  |Phi(z)| >= |z|.                       (SC2)
```

On every root-free boundary gap the radial boundary value of the real part in
`(SC1)` is zero, so the argument of `Phi` is constant and the gap maps to a
radial slit.  This is an exact structural coordinate, not a metric closure:
the missing theorem is a sharp pullback-length or safe-slit selector compatible
with `(SC2)`.  The Herglotz/Cayley boundary-root coordinate family was already
landed by `FullPassBatchAssimilation20260827.md`; the explicit `Phi` formula is
a useful normalization inside that family, not a new parent-facing theorem.

## Specialization collision: the returned lobe budget is already in `(LC2)`

Let `P` be squarefree and monic of degree `n`, let

```text
mu = min {|P(c)| : P'(c)=0} > 0,     rho = mu^(1/n),
```

and let `U_i` be the one-root components of `{|P|<mu}`, with root `a_i`.
Choose the inverse uniformization

```text
phi_i : D -> U_i,    P(phi_i(w)) = mu w,    phi_i(0)=a_i,
```

and put `r_i=|phi_i'(0)|=mu/|P'(a_i)|`.  Then

```text
sum_i r_i^2 <= rho^2 = mu^(2/n).                         (CR2)
```

Indeed, if `phi_i(w)=a_i+sum_(m>=1)b_(i,m)w^m`, the univalent
area formula gives

```text
Area(U_i)=pi sum_(m>=1) m |b_(i,m)|^2 >= pi r_i^2.
```

The `U_i` are pairwise disjoint, while Pólya's area-capacity inequality
and `cap({|P|<=mu})=mu^(1/n)` give

```text
pi sum_i r_i^2 <= Area({|P|<mu})
                <= pi cap({|P|<=mu})^2
                = pi mu^(2/n).
```

This proves `(CR2)`. Consequently every subset `I` of `d` lobes obeys

```text
sum_(i in I) r_i <= sqrt(d) rho.                         (CR1)
```

The derivation is correct, but it is not a new theorem in this corpus.  For
every regular `t<mu`, the singleton terms of Theorem A `(LC2)` in
`SixReturnJointAssimilation20260826.md` read

```text
sum_i t^2/|P'(a_i)|^2 < t^(2/n).
```

Letting `t` increase to `mu` gives `(CR2)` directly.  The source-current
theorem is strictly stronger because it holds at every regular level and, once
mergers occur, simultaneously retains the active nontrivial-component energy
and Bergman-demand terms.  The Cauchy consequence `(CR1)` is then immediate
from that already-active authority.

This collision was initially missed because assimilation compared theorem
wording and named routes but did not test whether a returned endpoint theorem
was a boundary specialization of a parameterized source-current result.  The
correction is fail-closed: `(CR2)` remains valid mathematics, but receives no
novelty or parent-progress credit.
