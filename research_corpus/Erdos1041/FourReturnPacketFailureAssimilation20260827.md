## Sharper proper-cluster consumer

Let

```text
p = q_S r_S,
q_S(z) = product_(i in S) (z-a_i),
r_S(z) = product_(j notin S) (z-a_j),
```

where `2 <= |S| = k < n`.  Let `C_S` be the convex hull of the selected roots,
and let `rho_S` be their minimum-enclosing-disc radius.  Assume the closed
degree-`k` Erdős 1041 theorem.

> **Common-envelope cluster certificate.** If
>
> ```text
> rho_S^k * max_(z in C_S) |r_S(z)| <= 1,
> ```
>
> then two roots in `S` are joined by a path of length at most `2 rho_S` in
> `{|p| <= 1}`.  If the roots are in the open unit disc and the displayed
> inequality is strict, the same path has length below two in `{|p| < 1}`.

Proof: scale the selected roots by their minimum enclosing disc and apply the
closed degree-`k` theorem to `q_S`.  Metric projection of the resulting path to
`C_S` fixes its endpoints, does not increase length, and decreases every
selected-root distance, so `|q_S| <= rho_S^k` still holds.  On the projected
path, `|r_S(z)|` is bounded by the single common-envelope maximum.  Multiplying
the two bounds proves containment.  Also `rho_S<1` for finitely many roots in
the open unit disc.

This strictly strengthens the return's condition

```text
rho_S^k * product_(j notin S) max_(i in S) |a_i-a_j| <= 1,
```

because for every `z in C_S`, convexity gives

```text
|r_S(z)| <= product_(j notin S) max_(i in S) |a_i-a_j|,
```

while the separate maxima need not occur at one common point.  Since `r_S` is
holomorphic, the maximum over a nondegenerate convex polygon occurs on its
boundary; the sharper certificate is therefore a boundary-envelope problem,
not an intractable interior search.

The theorem is still conditional and does not prove that every configuration
has a proper subset satisfying its hypothesis.  That cluster-selection
statement, or an exact counterexample to it, is the only reason to fund this
coordinate further.
