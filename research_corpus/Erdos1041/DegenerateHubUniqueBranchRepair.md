## Unique-branch lemma

Let `Delta` be a simply connected plane domain, let `v` lie in `Delta`, and let

```text
F : Omega -> Delta
```

be a finite proper holomorphic map of degree `d`, with `Omega` a connected
plane domain. Assume that `v` is the only critical value of `F`. If
`c in F^{-1}(v)` has local degree `m>=2`, then

```text
F^{-1}(v) = {c},        d=m,
```

and `c` is the only critical point of `F`.

## Proof

The fibre `E=F^{-1}(v)` is finite because `F` is finite proper. A plane domain
remains path-connected after deletion of finitely many points, so

```text
Omega* = Omega \ E
```

is connected. Since `v` is the only critical value, restriction gives an
unbranched connected covering

```text
F : Omega* -> Delta \ {v}
```

of degree `d`.

The punctured simply connected domain `Delta\{v}` has fundamental group
infinite cyclic. Hence the monodromy action on a regular fibre is generated by
one permutation `tau in S_d`. Connectedness of the covering is equivalent to
transitivity of the monodromy action. A cyclic permutation group generated by
`tau` is transitive on `d` letters exactly when `tau` is one `d`-cycle.

On the other hand, the cycle decomposition of local monodromy around `v` has
one cycle for every point `p in F^{-1}(v)`, and that cycle's length is the local
degree of `F` at `p`. Since `tau` is one `d`-cycle, the fibre over `v` contains
exactly one point, and its local degree is `d`. The fibre contains `c`, whose
local degree is `m`; therefore the unique point is `c` and `d=m`. Any critical
point maps to the only critical value `v`, so `c` is also the only critical
point. This proves the lemma.

## Consequence for the cyclic Blaschke form

Under Hypothesis (H) of `DegenerateHubBlaschkeReduction.md`, take `Omega` to be
the component of `f^{-1}(Delta)` containing `c`. Once the standard properness
of the polynomial restriction is established, the lemma applies. Thus the
transported map

```text
B = sigma^{-1} o f o psi : D -> D
```

has degree `m` and exactly one point over its sole branch value, with total
ramification at `0`. Removing that fibre gives the connected degree-`m`
covering of a punctured disc. Its deck group is cyclic and fixes `0`; after a
rotation it acts by `zeta -> omega zeta`. Consequently

```text
B(omega zeta)=B(zeta),
B(zeta)=b(zeta^m)
```

for a degree-one Blaschke map `b`. This is precisely the missing justification
for the normal form

```text
B(zeta)=(zeta^m-x^m)/(1-x^m zeta^m).
```

The earlier Riemann--Hurwitz sentence can be retained as a consistency check,
but it should follow this monodromy argument rather than assume its conclusion.
