## Exact surviving gap

The return confirms the same first unsupported implication already isolated by
the live corpus:

```text
strong global/full-fibre metric budget
  + exact safe root-attached arms
  + exact component/Reeb topology
  -/-> one attachment-compatible root pair joined within the same 2R budget.
```

The required new mathematics is therefore not another proof search through the
dossiers.  It is a construction theorem that converts full-fibre or scalar
budget into one merging pair without losing monodromy, component identity, or
external-root coupling.  Equivalent target-deciding constructions include:

1. a critical-angle localization theorem selecting two short arms that merge;
2. the complete componentwise charge/Blaschke trace inequality;
3. an attachment-aware connector that spends a quantified slack source; or
4. COVER (or a stronger sufficient consumer) with its hypotheses discharged.

## Packet-construction lesson

This return obeyed honesty constraints but still spent substantial effort
checking whether the complete proof was already present.  That is not a valid
research objective for a mature open frontier.  A successor packet must begin
at the first unsupported edge above, treat corpus discovery as preflight only,
and demand construction of the missing implication.

An incomplete return is useful only if it lands new authority at that edge: a
proved bridge lemma, an exact counterexample/no-go that deletes a proposed
bridge, a quantitatively stronger producer consumed by the parent chain, or a
strictly smaller residual with an executable next construction.  “The proof
was not found in the supplied corpus or outside work” is not a mathematical
landing and must not satisfy the packet.

## Frontier decision

Keep combined charge, attachment-aware localization/COVER, and complete
consumer construction as the target-deciding frontier.  Do not dispatch
another packet whose principal action is corpus search, literature search, or
rederivation of the full-fibre action and reciprocal identities.  Those are
premises now; the next packet starts after them.
