## 1. Executive judgment

**#249 is one of the more strongly anchored problems in this corpus — there are at least five
statements a stranger can check from the statement alone.** The single best one is the explicit
denominator exclusion: if `S = a/q` then `q > 79 639 646 646 701 375 323 355 774 875 831 053`
(≈ 7.96 × 10³⁴), Lean-checked and sorry-free, and *sharp for the window that produces it* — the
next integer up is proved to be the exact first denominator at which that window's certificate
fails. That is a `q > 10^N`-shaped anchor with a sharpness receipt attached, which the corpus's
other record bounds do not have. The strongest *unpackaged* result is different and arguably more
interesting to a specialist: **for every integer `r ≥ 1`, `Θ_r · Θ_{r+2} < Θ_{r+1}²` where
`Θ_r = ∑_{d≥1} μ(d)/(2^d−1)^r`** — strict log-concavity of the entire Möbius–Mersenne ladder, hence
every shifted 2×2 Hankel determinant strictly negative. It needs no #249 vocabulary at all, it is
sorry-free, and it appears in **neither the paper, nor the public monolith, nor any
`ExternalVerification249*` Comparator directory**. Third is the sharp rank-one Schur floor, which
carries a genuine best constant (`21/320`) plus an equality case and a 1/16-vs-1/15 optimality
boundary. Two further anchors are clean closed-form evaluations that read as competition-grade
identities. The main prior-art hazards are concentrated in the *rank/basis* lane (Martin 2006 is
the independence input and the paper says so) and in the *periodic-weight* lane (Luca–Tachiya 2017
Theorem 1 already proves it, and the paper concedes this). The Farey-mediant method behind the
record bound is classical; the specific constant is what is ours.
