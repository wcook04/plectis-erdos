## 1. The shorter consumer

Let

\[
 Q_n=\left\lfloor\frac{2^n}{21}\right\rfloor-
       \sum_{m\le n} f(m)2^{n-m}
\]

be the natural denominator-21 greedy defect.  Its exact recurrence is

\[
 Q_n=2Q_{n-1}+t_n-f(n),
\]

where `t_n` is the periodic binary digit of `1/21`.  If `n>0` and
`Q_n<=Q_(n-1)`, then

\[
 Q_{n-1}\le f(n)\le n,
 \qquad Q_n\le n.
\]

The first inequality uses only `t_n>=0`; the second is the universal divisor
bound `f(n)<=n`.  Therefore a non-rise at every positive multiple of `120`
gives cofinally many linear defect and carry returns.  The already checked
tempered-carry endpoint then yields

\[
 \bigl(\forall k\ge0,\;Q_{120(k+1)}\le Q_{120(k+1)-1}\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow \frac1{21}\in\mathcal A.
\]

This strictly simplifies the route in `RecordCofinitenessBoundary.md`: the
eventual-record theorem is unnecessary once one observes that a single
non-rise itself bounds the defect by its rank.

## 2. The finite past-owned producer

Let `A` be the actual greedy support for `1/21`.  At a positive multiple `n`
of `120`, define

\[
 U_{30}(n)=\#\{d\in A:\ d\mid n/2\text{ or }d\mid n/3
                         \text{ or }d\mid n/5\}.
\]

Every counted rank is at most `n/2`, hence its greedy decision is already
owned by the past.  Lean formalizes this as a literal union of three finite
selected-divisor sets and proves

\[
 U_{30}(n)\le f(n).
\]

It also proves the subtraction-free seven-column identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_{30}(n)&+f(n/6)+f(n/10)+f(n/15)\\
 &=f(n/2)+f(n/3)+f(n/5)+f(n/30).
\end{aligned}
\]

The periodic baseline bit immediately before `n=120(k+1)` is exactly one.
Consequently the open producer

\[
 \tag{PU120}
 U_{30}(120(k+1))\ge Q_{120(k+1)-1}+1
\]

implies `Q_(120(k+1))<=Q_(120(k+1)-1)`, hence `1/21` membership.  This entire
producer-to-endpoint chain is now represented in Lean; only the all-depth
inequality `(PU120)` remains unproved.

### The doubled-row excess layer

The cockpit's redirect from holes to excess multiplicity has an exact form
inside this socket.  For a positive rank `a`, put

\[
 g(a)=f(2a)-f(a),
\]

the number of selected divisors which appear for the first time after
doubling `a`.  Lean defines this without natural-number subtraction as a
finite-set difference.  Applying the seven-column identity at `n=240m` and
cancelling the old divisor layers gives

\[
 \boxed{U_{30}(240m)+g(8m)=f(120m)+g(40m)+g(24m).} \tag{DL240}
\]

Thus the extra payment on doubled rows is not a diffuse density statistic.
It is the signed balance of three explicit new `2`-adic layers.  This is the
precise bridge between the shift-window excess-multiplicity coordinate and
the source-owned divisor ancestry used by `(PU120)`.

The exact receipt also tests the stronger cofinal producer

\[
 \tag{PU240+}
 U_{30}(240m)\ge Q_{240m-1}+2.
\]

All `8,333` audited doubled rows satisfy it, with minimum margin one relative
to `(PU120)` and no equality.  Grouping all `16,666` modulus-`120` rows by the
cofactor `m` gives: one unit row with margin two; `1,928` prime rows with
minimum margin zero and the three equalities above; and `14,737` composite
rows with minimum margin one and no equality.  This is an exact finite
discriminator, not a proof that composite cofactors always buy one unit.

Lean checks that `(PU240+)` alone implies `1/21` membership: the doubled rows
are already cofinal, the extra unit is more than the current baseline pulse,
and each resulting non-rise gives a linear carry return.  The new candidate
is therefore a genuinely smaller row family than `(PU120)`, but its all-depth
inequality remains a complete counterexample producer, not a cheap corollary
of cofactor arithmetic.

### Exact factor-descent falsifier

The most economical attempted proof of the composite-cofactor slack is false.
Put

\[
 M(m)=U_{30}(120m)-Q_{120m-1}-1.
\]

One could hope that adjoining a prime factor never decreases `M`, or at least
that every composite `m` has some prime divisor `p` with
`M(m)>=M(m/p)`.  The regenerated exact orbit through rank `2,000,002`
tests every such prime-deletion edge among the audited composite cofactors:

- `39,516` exact edges are tested;
- `119` decrease;
- the minimum difference `M(m)-M(m/p)` is `-3`, at
  `m=16358`, `p=2`;
- four cofactors decrease against **every** prime-deletion parent:
  `32`, `5438`, `6374`, and `15854`;
- the latter three have child margin exactly `1`, the minimum observed
  composite slack.

For example,

\[
 M(5438)=1<M(2719)=3,
 \qquad M(5438)=1<M(2)=2.
\]

Thus neither monotonic factor descent nor an existential nondecreasing-parent
induction can prove `(PU240+)`.  This is finite exact falsification of those
named lemmas, not a failure of `(PU240+)` itself: every audited doubled row
still has the required unit slack.  A surviving proof must compare the gain
in selected divisor layers with the **simultaneous change in the live defect**;
static cofactor ancestry alone loses precisely that coupling.

### Coupled factor descent survives only the original horizon

Retaining that coupling produces a sharply different result.  Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(m)&=f(120m)+g(40m)+g(24m),\\
 D(m)&=Q_{240m-1}+g(8m).
\end{aligned}
\]

Identity `(DL240)` makes `(PU240+)` exactly

\[
 D(m)+2\le P(m). \tag{CD240}
\]

The source now packages the following strong-induction reduction.  Suppose
every composite `m != 16` has a proper divisor `a` such that

\[
 P(a)+D(m)\le P(m)+D(a),
\]

and suppose `(CD240)` holds at `m=1`, `m=16`, and every prime `m`.  Then
`(CD240)` holds for every positive `m`, so `(PU240+)` and `1/21` membership
follow.  The proof is a literal strong induction and uses no future-tail or
membership assumption.

The exact discriminator chooses the deterministic parent obtained by deleting
the largest prime factor.  Through all `8,333` audited cofactors it finds:

- `7,287` composite factor edges;
- exactly one decrease of `P-D`, at `m=16 -> 8`;
- zero decreases away from `16`, with minimum difference zero;
- direct balance `P(16)-D(16)=6`, versus the required two;
- `1,046` unit-or-prime boundary rows, with minimum balance two;
- boundary equality only at primes `2719`, `3187`, `7717`, and `7927`.

Thus the static margin descent is false, while the **coupled** payment/debt
descent survived every named test through the original rank `2,000,002` and
reduced the doubled-row problem to prime cofactors plus one finite socket.
The deeper computation below shows that the prime boundary itself is false.

### The prime boundary is one finite pullback column

The prime boundary itself now has an exact normal form.  Let `p>5` be prime,
pull the actual support back through multiplication by `p`, and let

\[
 C=\operatorname{Div}(120)\cup\{16,48,80\}.
\]

This is a fixed set of nineteen coefficients.  The prime dilation identity
splits every load at `p*c` into its old `c`-column and the pulled-back column.
Three-set inclusion-exclusion then proves, without subtraction,

\[
 P(p)+g(8)=P(1)+\#\{c\in C:cp\in A\}+g(8p). \tag{PC240}
\]

The last term occurs on both sides of `(CD240)`.  Consequently, for every
prime `p>5`, the prime boundary is equivalent to

\[
 Q_{240p-1}+g(8)+2
 \le P(1)+\#\{c\in C:cp\in A\}. \tag{PCB}
\]

Lean native-certifies the equivalent base identity `U_30(240)=7` from the
exact rational greedy prefix through rank `240`; its axiom audit therefore
includes the standard `native_decide` evaluation axiom.  Thus the prime repair is
the especially transparent equivalence

\[
 U_{30}(240p)=7+\#\{c\in C:cp\in A\},\qquad
 (PU240+)_p\Longleftrightarrow
 Q_{240p-1}\le \#\{c\in C:cp\in A\}+5.
\]

This is a real coordinate reduction: the seven moving divisor loads in the
original payment/debt expression have become one Boolean word of length
nineteen.  The receipt checks `(PC240)` at all `1,042` primes `5<p<=8333`
with zero failures.  The four tight boundary words are:

| `p` | `Q_(240p-1)` | selected `c` with `cp in A` |
|---:|---:|---|
| 2719 | 11 | `1,10,16,24,40,80` |
| 3187 | 9 | `6,15,20,60` |
| 7717 | 10 | `5,30,48,80,120` |
| 7927 | 8 | `1,4,120` |

All four tight primes are `1 mod 6`.  The named phase refinement

\[
 p\equiv5\pmod6\Longrightarrow
 Q_{240p-1}\le\#\{c\in C:cp\in A\}+4
\]

also has zero violations in the receipt: its `527` rows have minimum coupled
balance three, while the `515` rows at `p=1 mod 6` have minimum balance two.
That extra unit is finite evidence only.  It identifies the next analytic
question as a phase-sensitive selected-column inequality, not a denominator
or average-density estimate.

### The deeper orbit falsifies the prime-column bound

The nineteen-column identity survives; its proposed inequality does not.
The first `(PU240+)` violation is

\[
 n=5{,}296{,}080=240\cdot 22{,}067,
\]

where `22067` is prime.  The exact state is

\[
 Q_{n-1}=12,\qquad
 \{c\in C:22067c\in A\}=\{4,6,15,20,40,48\}.
\]

Thus the pullback load is six and the formally isolated boundary reads
`12 <= 6+5`, which is false by one.  This is exactly the theorem hypothesis
`TwentyOnePrimePullbackColumnBound`, so the deeper counterexample closes both
the prime boundary and the coupled factor-descent route regardless of whether
the composite descent step itself could be proved.

The broader certified audit through rank `20,000,002` finds `17` violations
of `(PU120)` (minimum margin `-3`) and `11` violations of `(PU240+)` (minimum
margin `-4`).  Thirteen of the seventeen violating `(PU120)` cofactors are
prime.  This is decisive negative evidence, not a horizon extension of a
surviving conjecture.

## 5. Closure and redirect

Do not spend another proof wave on `(PU120)`, `(PU240+)`, coupled factor
descent, or `TwentyOnePrimePullbackColumnBound`: explicit canonical
counterexamples exclude them.  The useful residue is the exact prime-column

producers already present in the packet.  They allow occasional negative
primorial margins and ask only for sufficiently many returns of the
renormalized defect.  This matches the independent denominator-cancellation
audit: the raw remainder denominator is genuinely quadratic, so a successful
argument must remain in quotient/defect or return-time coordinates rather than
seek a sharper estimate of the same rational remainder.
