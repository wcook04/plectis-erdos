\paragraph{Countable construction with a tail budget.}
Let $\mathcal H$ be the hereditary finite-union class generated by the
weighted and strengthened-cover criteria. For every sequence
$E_1,E_2,\ldots\in\mathcal H$, there are increasing cutoffs $s_i$ such that
\[
 A=\bigcup_{i\ge1}\bigl(E_i\cap(s_i,\infty)\bigr)
\]
has arbitrarily small positive displacements and every infinite subset
of $A$ has irrational subseries at every integer base.
At stage $j$, choose a return $N_j$, divisible by $j!$, for the first
$j$ truncated hosts, with displacement less than $2^{-j-2}$.
Require $s_{j+1}\ge N_j+j+3$. Every later exponent is then beyond that
cutoff and its total contribution at $N_j$ is at most
\[
 (2^{N_j}-1)\sum_{a>s_{j+1}}\frac1{2^a-1}
 \le2^{N_j+1-s_{j+1}}\le2^{-j-2}.
\]
This preserves the previous return. Infinitely many distinct prime-power
centres yield such a host outside every finite weighted-plus-cover
splitting, even after finitely many fixed dilates of prime-power supports
are adjoined. The logarithmic obstruction is stable under deleting a
reciprocal-summable set; this supplies the decomposition-independent
separation in the companion proof.

\paragraph{An actual failure of synchronisation.}
The primes can be partitioned into two reciprocal-divergent supports,
each having arbitrarily small displacements along every prescribed
modulus, while their union has displacement greater than $1/3$ at every
positive shift. Alternately assign a fresh prime block of reciprocal
mass at least one to one colour. Choose a multiple $N$ of all primes
already assigned that colour and of the required factorial. Assign all
remaining primes through $N+k+3$ to the other colour. Later assignments
contribute at most $2^{-k-2}$ at this return. Each colour receives
infinitely many mass-one blocks. The prime-core bound prevents a common
small return. Separate actual return theorems therefore do not replace
the common averaging estimate.
