# The organising idea

A positive displacement has two separate requirements: the selected finite part must return, and the as-yet uncontrolled tail must remain small at that same time. The accepted mixed theorem aligns two error estimates on one finite distribution. The new countable construction instead chooses the next support cutoff after choosing the current return time. The exact budget is

\[
\Delta_A(N)\le\Delta_B(N)+(2^N-1)X_{A\setminus B}(2),
\qquad
A\setminus B\subseteq(R,\infty)\Longrightarrow
\Delta_{A\setminus B}(N)\le2^{N+1-R}\quad(R\ge N).
\]

That distinction explains both the extension and the failure. Countably many accepted mixed hosts can be included modulo finite deletions. An infinite family of distinct prime-power centres gives a resulting host outside *every* finite weighted-plus-cover decomposition, even after finitely many fixed dilates of prime-power supports are adjoined. Conversely, two actual prime supports can separately have arbitrarily small returns, along every prescribed modulus, while their union has displacement greater than 1/3 everywhere.

## What is new in this return

Research Theorem 2.3 proves delayed countable gluing. Lemma 3.1 makes the logarithmic obstruction stable under reciprocal-summable erasure. Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4 use that stability to prove strict separation from the entire finite mixed class and its finite prime-power augmentation. Theorem 5.3 is an actual-support replacement for the abstract alternating-functions countermodel. Proposition 6.1 identifies the return class as residual; this is a standard Baire argument with a stronger hereditary quantifier, not a claim to have originated generic irrationality. Even the hereditary generic value statement follows from strict-tail injectivity and countability; the additional certificate here is actual return-admissibility.

These are ordinary proofs for independent adjudication. Their historical novelty has not been established. The two previously accepted inputs are the r5 mixed synchronisation theorem and the r4 first-logarithmic-moment obstruction. They are stated explicitly in Section 1 and are not counted as new.

## A useful correction to the hierarchy

The mixed criterion logically includes the reciprocal-summable flagship: choose any nonempty finite prime set P and use h/(2^h-1) <= 1, so W_P(A) <= sum(1/a), with an empty cover component. Keeping the flagship as the lead is justified by its economical statement, direct proof, and formal status. It should not be justified by asserting a false logical noncontainment. This change does not retitle the note or promote a Palomar claim.

## Selective adversarial conclusions

The baseline proof survives; the proposed direct all-base version supplies both required dominations. The accepted finite kernel survives the three-regime argument and gives the finite cover proof without a limiting exchange. The new countable proof survives because future exponents are uniformly beyond the protected cutoff, rather than merely assigned to components with separate return theorems. The strict-separation proof survives arbitrary alternative splittings: the weighted part must have finite reciprocal mass on an unrecognised centre, and the erasure lemma leaves an unbounded logarithmic moment for the cover part. The untruncated union inference fails on actual prime supports, not only on unrelated test functions.
