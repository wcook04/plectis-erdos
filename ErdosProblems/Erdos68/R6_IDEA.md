# The idea, and where it should appear

The divisor basis already separates the channel equations. The new step is to separate the recurrence's *finite initial sources* from its already-controlled tail. To certify an infinite gcd, it is enough to prove that the initial sources vanish modulo the proposed gcd after the computed prefix. Unordered-block divisibility gives them a finite factorial cutoff, and their sums can cancel before their individual terms vanish. This gives a quadratic universal prefix bound and shorter exact certificates. The same closure argument turns a prescribed remote-support constraint into a finite integer-lattice problem with a computable exact minimum moment.

For the short note, show the residual identity immediately after the low-channel classification. It tells the reader why the next arithmetic optimisation matters: it determines the admissible multiplier, while every zero-moment repair changes the residual by an integer. The new finite-source theorem can then replace the longer leading gcd argument after adjudication. The remote lattice theorem belongs initially in the mathematical record, with one small dual-certificate example; it should not become another opening inventory.

The result-facing lead stays: two incomparable exclusions on rational representations of the actual series. Its elementary factorial-divisibility consumer should finish on page one. The first numbered companion theorem and its complete proof remain unchanged. There is no new bound on the denominator of the original series in this round.

The structural gain is specific. An unrestricted arithmetic optimisation over all finite supports has an explicit finite certificate, including simultaneous low-channel cancellation and an arbitrary forbidden initial segment. The unresolved real task is still the signed-block inequality or the simultaneous collision/complementary-residue inequality. Neither is supplied by solving the integer constraints.
