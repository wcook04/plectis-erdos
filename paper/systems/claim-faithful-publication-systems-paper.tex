Plectis organises mathematical source, evidence and exposition into a persistent problem-sized world whose main reader-facing outputs are a short paper and a complete reasoning record.
% END SENTENCE p7.001

We describe which parts of that route exist in the attached source snapshot, which require editorial judgement, and which remain interface contracts.

\section{A research record that can become a paper}\label{sec:intro}

% SENTENCE p7.008
The practical question is how an existing mathematical corpus becomes a paper that another mathematician can understand, check and reuse.
% END SENTENCE p7.008
% SENTENCE p7.009
For this purpose a problem-sized world contains the problem, established results, scoped failed routes, literature, proof evidence and open obligations, rather than only a list of successful declarations.
% END SENTENCE p7.009
% SENTENCE p7.010
The short paper carries the mathematical argument a reader should remember, while the long record preserves the fuller development and provenance.
% END SENTENCE p7.010

% SENTENCE p7.011
Problem 257 supplies a concrete example: for a finite nonempty set of primes $P$, write $h_P(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}$ and $X_A(b)=\sum_{a\in A}(b^a-1)^{-1}$.
% END SENTENCE p7.011
% SENTENCE p7.012
The short paper states that convergence of $\sum_{a\in A}h_P(a)/[a(2^{h_P(a)}-1)]$ implies irrationality of $X_B(b)$ for every integer $b\ge2$ and every infinite $B\subseteq A$.
% END SENTENCE p7.012
% SENTENCE p7.013
Its proof makes positive displacements small by averaging over multiples of a modulus, with a further dyadic average controlling incomplete periods.
% END SENTENCE p7.013
% SENTENCE p7.014
The universal infinite-support problem remains outside that sufficient criterion, and failure of the criterion is not evidence of rationality.
% END SENTENCE p7.014

% SENTENCE p7.015
This example fixes the writing objective: preserve the general theorem, make the hard step intelligible, distinguish it from antecedents, and state the remaining problem exactly.
% END SENTENCE p7.015
% SENTENCE p7.016
It supplies no measurement of whether Plectis made the theorem easier to discover or understand.
% END SENTENCE p7.016

\section{Pre-digestion: selecting a usable result}\label{sec:predigestion}\label{sec:world}\label{sec:graph}

% SENTENCE p1.001
The existing problem index joins authored questions and boundaries to derived module paths, paper routes, digests and recorded checking dispositions.
% END SENTENCE p1.001
% SENTENCE p1.002
The argument-continuation machinery builds a graph from an elaborated export and records the source identity and limits of that export.
% END SENTENCE p1.002
% SENTENCE p1.003
A relation row distinguishes a replacement hypothesis, an endpoint route and an equivalence inside the stated context.
% END SENTENCE p1.003
% SENTENCE p1.004
An atlas listing with a matching signature and a reachable build target is an attestation to the source projection; it does not establish that a particular continuous-integration run compiled the module.
% END SENTENCE p1.004

% SENTENCE p1.005
The round contract proposes a per-problem dossier containing each result's statement, generality, mechanism, hard step, evidence, consumers, attribution and open questions.
% END SENTENCE p1.005
% SENTENCE p1.006
That dossier should be an editorial selection over the existing corpus, with a reason for retaining or excluding each candidate result.
% END SENTENCE p1.006
% SENTENCE p1.007
Retrieval alone cannot establish that a result is the most useful abstraction or that the literature comparison is complete.
% END SENTENCE p1.007
% SENTENCE p1.008
The proposed acceptance test therefore requires an exact source statement, a supported generality relation and a named potential consumer, with missing information left explicit.
% END SENTENCE p1.008
% SENTENCE p1.009
This report does not treat the parallel dossier implementation as already delivered.
% END SENTENCE p1.009

\section{Writing the short paper}\label{sec:short}\label{sec:production}

% SENTENCE p3.001
The supplied essays motivate this objective from different perspectives: Kra asks what a result teaches, Riehl emphasises explanatory generality, Cohn treats undigested output as a burden on readers, and Sanderson argues for recognising motivated explanations~\cite{essays}.
% END SENTENCE p3.001
% SENTENCE p3.002
Gowers distinguishes legitimate problem-solving and theory-building aims, so these views provide editorial questions rather than a unanimous policy about AI-assisted mathematics~\cite{essays}.
% END SENTENCE p3.002

% SENTENCE p3.003
The supplied private machinery contains a source-frozen editorial packet builder and a writing brief that prioritises the strongest established contribution, its antecedents and its hard idea.
% END SENTENCE p3.003
% SENTENCE p3.004
This is machinery for assigning and packaging editorial work; the attached source does not establish a complete automatic writer from the new dossier contract.
% END SENTENCE p3.004
% SENTENCE p3.005
The builder also imports a dissemination helper that is absent from the supplied private archive, so this report does not claim a successful offline run of that builder.
% END SENTENCE p3.005

% SENTENCE p3.006
The proposed short-paper writer consumes the dossier and claim-evidence rows, then drafts a theorem-led account in which the motivating problem is an application of the general result.
% END SENTENCE p3.006
% SENTENCE p3.007
The drafting order should be mathematical question, general statement with hypotheses, mechanism, hard step, comparison with antecedents, and the exact open boundary.
% END SENTENCE p3.007
% SENTENCE p3.008
For the weighted-support example, the short paper should explain why incomplete periods require the second average before listing supplementary finite-denominator results.
% END SENTENCE p3.008
% SENTENCE p3.009
The long record can retain those supplementary developments without making the short paper an inventory of everything that was proved.
% END SENTENCE p3.009

% SENTENCE p3.010
A structural linter can detect missing evidence fields, unsupported status changes and forbidden register patterns, while explanatory adequacy still needs a reader.
% END SENTENCE p3.010
% SENTENCE p3.011
A successful revision should enable a cold reader to recover the statement, its mechanism, its difficult step and its limitations without consulting the originating conversation.
% END SENTENCE p3.011

\section{Assembling the long record}\label{sec:long}

% SENTENCE p4.001
The native assembler writes flat long manuscripts from authored reasoning parts and retains a visible marker before each part.
% END SENTENCE p4.001
% SENTENCE p4.002
Its check mode compares the generated manuscript with the assembled result and acts as a freshness gate.
% END SENTENCE p4.002
% SENTENCE p4.003
This arrangement lets a reviewer revise a bounded part while keeping a manuscript that ordinary TeX and text-extraction tools can read.
% END SENTENCE p4.003

% SENTENCE p4.004
Assembly freshness does not imply that the long record agrees mathematically with the short paper.
% END SENTENCE p4.004
% SENTENCE p4.005
The proposed cross-paper check should join stable result identifiers and compare statements, assumptions, evidence classes and open boundaries before either manuscript is released.
% END SENTENCE p4.005
% SENTENCE p4.006
A deletion from the short paper should preserve the long-record result or record an explicit correction; it should not erase the history of a failed route.
% END SENTENCE p4.006
% SENTENCE p4.007
The short and long versions may use different explanations while citing the same supported mathematical assertion.
% END SENTENCE p4.007

\section{The review and improvement loop}\label{sec:loop}\label{sec:cycle}

\papersectiontarget{systems-research-loop}\papersectiontarget{systems-public}
% SENTENCE p5.001
The public research record maintains an append-only hash-linked journal of packets, returns, reviews and consumer dispositions.
% END SENTENCE p5.001
% SENTENCE p5.002
The supplied private intake tool preserves return bytes before judgement, and its reflection tool consumes outcome records separately from custody.
% END SENTENCE p5.002
% SENTENCE p5.003
The reflection tool can examine outcomes, policies, mathematics, context, assimilation, infrastructure and objectives, while changes to directives and objectives remain operator proposals.
% END SENTENCE p5.003
% SENTENCE p5.004
Its documented promotion rule requires a causal-trial contract, evidence from separate batches and a clean replay before a candidate policy becomes active.
% END SENTENCE p5.004

% SENTENCE p5.005
For paper work, the proposed loop is to freeze inputs, commission a bounded revision, preserve its return, check changed claims, review its exposition, and either integrate or reject the change with reasons.
% END SENTENCE p5.005
% SENTENCE p5.006
An accepted component still needs a recorded consumer and an integration decision; acceptance does not itself show that a paper changed or a reader benefited.
% END SENTENCE p5.006
% SENTENCE p5.007
Policy changes should be tested against retained difficult cases as well as the latest successful return, because making a prompt stricter can suppress useful work.
% END SENTENCE p5.007
% SENTENCE p5.008
The existence of repeated rounds is evidence of an iterative process, not evidence that the process improves automatically.
% END SENTENCE p5.008

\section{Evaluation through ordinary research packets}\label{sec:evaluation}

% SENTENCE p6.001
The existing semantic benchmark runs fixed queries and checks for expected source handles, statuses and dependency entries.
% END SENTENCE p6.001
% SENTENCE p6.002
Such a check can reveal broken navigation, but it does not measure reader understanding or the usefulness of a paper.
% END SENTENCE p6.002
% SENTENCE p6.003
The native reader-study tool prepares digest-bound materials and analysis, and its optional execution path requires a separately authorised provider bridge.
% END SENTENCE p6.003
% SENTENCE p6.004
The operator's revised protocol uses the ordinary Type B research sessions as subjects and requires no paid model calls or new provider runner.
% END SENTENCE p6.004

% SENTENCE p6.005
For a component comparison, both packets receive the same task, source revision, evidence boundary, budget and frozen rubric, with one declared input difference.
% END SENTENCE p6.005
% SENTENCE p6.006
The corresponding source-only control and structured-dossier treatment should contain the same mathematical facts when the estimand is the effect of organisation.
% END SENTENCE p6.006
% SENTENCE p6.007
A separate grader receives anonymised outputs in counterbalanced order, together with the frozen answer key and rubric; it receives neither the producing arm label nor the desired result.
% END SENTENCE p6.007
% SENTENCE p6.008
Severe mathematical or evidence-status errors are reported separately from exposition scores and cannot be cancelled by fluent prose.
% END SENTENCE p6.008

% SENTENCE p6.009
The primary observations are whether a reader can state the general result, identify the hard step, locate its evidence and distinguish the remaining open question.
% END SENTENCE p6.009
% SENTENCE p6.010
Related short and long papers, repeated sessions and equivalent formulations are clustered by mathematical lineage instead of being treated as independent discoveries.
% END SENTENCE p6.010
% SENTENCE p6.011
Every issued assignment, missing return, timeout, invalid output and adjudication should remain in the record, together with preparation and review effort.
% END SENTENCE p6.011
% SENTENCE p6.012
Ordinary session access avoids additional model-call charges in this design, while human time and any existing subscription remain costs.
% END SENTENCE p6.012
% SENTENCE p6.013
A before-and-after writing round can show revision progress, whereas a causal claim about a component requires the matched comparison rather than different tasks assigned to different components.
% END SENTENCE p6.013
% SENTENCE p6.014
The protocol would count against the upgrade if source-faithful reader decisions did not improve, if severe errors increased, or if additional preparation outweighed the recorded benefit.
% END SENTENCE p6.014
% SENTENCE p6.015
No comparative scores or reader gains are reported for this revision.
% END SENTENCE p6.015

\section{Relation to prior systems}\label{sec:related}

% SENTENCE p7.026
LeanArchitect is the nearest listed comparator for synchronising exposition with formal declarations: its annotations and environment data support dependency extraction and generated blueprint text~\cite{leanarchitect}.
% END SENTENCE p7.026
% SENTENCE p7.027
Plectis adds a different publication path around mixed evidence classes, separately recorded statement comparison, short and long paper roles, and the custody of unresolved returned work.
% END SENTENCE p7.027
% SENTENCE p7.028
This combination has not been shown to produce clearer papers or better formalisation performance than LeanArchitect.
% END SENTENCE p7.028

% SENTENCE p7.029
The Equational Theories Project is a stronger precedent for demonstrated collaborative exploration, including implication and non-implication data, Lean validation and shared project management~\cite{etp}.
% END SENTENCE p7.029
% SENTENCE p7.030
Persistence, correction and formal checking therefore cannot serve as standalone novelty claims for Plectis.
% END SENTENCE p7.030
% SENTENCE p7.031
The mathlib community supplies the reusable formal library and maintenance model on which problem-specific developments can build, while Formal Conjectures supplies formal problem statements~\cite{mathlib,formalconjectures}.
% END SENTENCE p7.031

% SENTENCE p7.032
AlphaEvolve improves code against executable evaluators and stores evaluated candidates in an evolutionary database, while its mathematical exploration report studies constructions and subsequent mathematical analysis~\cite{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath}.
% END SENTENCE p7.032
% SENTENCE p7.033
The present paper-writing loop does not demonstrate comparable autonomous optimisation, and grammatical quality alone is not a trustworthy executable evaluator of mathematical exposition.
% END SENTENCE p7.033
% SENTENCE p7.034
Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics proposes proof-length-based interestingness and studies its relation to downstream utility in formal libraries~\cite{interestingness}.
% END SENTENCE p7.034
% SENTENCE p7.035
Plectis treats such a score as a possible selection signal whose value for a short paper would still need a named consumer and a reader test.
% END SENTENCE p7.035

% SENTENCE p7.036
Agent Hunt distributes autoformalisation through bounties, OpenProver maintains planner, worker and verifier state, and OProver incorporates retrieval and compiler feedback into formal proving~\cite{agenthunt,openprover,oprover}.
% END SENTENCE p7.036
% SENTENCE p7.037
CircuitProver explicitly studies reusable proof knowledge in hardware verification, so storing reusable lemmas is also an established direction~\cite{circuitprover}.
% END SENTENCE p7.037
% SENTENCE p7.038
The differentiating question here is how the resulting research record becomes trustworthy exposition, not whether this workflow proves more theorems than those systems.
% END SENTENCE p7.038

% SENTENCE p7.039
RE-Bench illustrates the importance of explicit tasks, budgets and comparators in evaluating research agents; its official versioned abstract is used here because the attached extraction includes an earlier internal draft~\cite{rebench}.
% END SENTENCE p7.039
% SENTENCE p7.040
Halmos\textquotesingle{} account of mathematical writing supports treating the organisation of an argument as substantive work rather than assuming a proof transcript is already a finished paper~\cite{halmos}.
% END SENTENCE p7.040
