\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Erd\H{o}s~\#249 asks whether $\Stot=\sum_{n\ge1}\ph(n)/2^n$ is irrational.
It is open, and nothing here closes it.  What is established, unconditionally
and with machine-checked proofs, is the following.  For every $e\ge0$ the
canonical level-$e$ family of dyadic totient sections $n\mapsto\ph(2^jn+r)$ is
linearly independent over $\Q$ with span of \emph{exact} dimension $2^e+1$;
consequently the span of all dyadic sections of $\ph$ is not
finite-dimensional over $\Q$.  Separately, $\Stot\neq p/q$ for every
$q\le 79\,639\,646\,646\,701\,375\,323\,355\,774\,875\,831\,053$.

We also record the exact identity $\Stot=\sum_{d\ge1}\Aw(d)/(2^d-1)$ with
$\Aw=\ph*\mu\ge0$, $\Aw(p)=p-2$, $\Aw(p^e)=(p-1)^2p^{e-2}$.  This places
\#249 in one family with three series that \emph{are} settled --- the
Erd\H{o}s--Borwein constant, and, since December 2025, the $\omega$ and
$\Omega$ series of Tao and Ter\"av\"ainen --- and isolates one difference:
those three have a $0/1$ Lambert weight, and $\Aw$ is unbounded.  For
\emph{eventually periodic} nonnegative weights the irrationality conclusion is
itself machine-checked here in every integer base $b\ge2$; $\Aw$ is not
eventually periodic, and that gap is the problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The four series, and which are settled}\label{sec:family}

If $f=g*\mathbf 1$, that is $f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}g(d)$, then interchanging the
two sums gives
\[
  \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{f(n)}{2^n}
  \;=\;\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{g(d)}{2^d-1}.
  \tag{1}\label{eq:lambert}
\]
Four classical coefficient sequences are of this shape.  The table records the
resulting constant, the weight $g=f*\mu$ it corresponds to, and its status.
Every decimal below was recomputed to $50$ places for this note.

\noindent The first three weights take only the values $0$ and $1$.  The
fourth does not: $\Aw(p)=p-2$ is unbounded.  That is the only structural
difference between \#249 and its three settled neighbours, and it is not a
cosmetic one --- the settled cases are all instances of Erd\H{o}s~\#257, the
question of which \emph{supports} $A$ make $\sum_{a\in A}(2^a-1)^{-1}$
irrational, whereas \#249 is a single unbounded-weight member of the same
family.

\begin{proposition}[the \#249 weight]\label{res:weight}
$\Stot=\sum_{d\ge1}\Aw(d)/(2^d-1)$, where $\Aw=\ph*\mu$ satisfies $\Aw\ge0$,
$\Aw(p)=p-2$ and $\Aw(p^e)=(p-1)^2p^{e-2}$ for $e\ge2$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is~\eqref{eq:lambert} at $f=\ph$, using $\ph=\Aw*\mathbf 1$; it
is checked as the
\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18117}{tsum_primWeight_div_two_pow_sub_one_eq_totient_series}{positive-weight lift}.
Two further rungs of the same ladder are exact rationals: $\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)=\tfrac12$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18106}{tsum_moebius_div_two_pow_sub_one_eq_half}{Lean})
and $\sum_{d\ge1}\ph(d)/(2^d-1)=2$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18101}{tsum_totient_div_two_pow_sub_one_eq_two}{Lean}),
and there is a signed squared-Mersenne lens
$\Stot=\tfrac12+\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)^2$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18126}{totient_series_eq_half_add_moebius_mersenne_square}{Lean}).
The difficulty of \#249 therefore does not live in the Lambert transform,
where the neighbouring values are rational and exactly computable; it lives on
the power-series side.

\paragraph{Why the unboundedness is the obstruction, precisely.}
For nonnegative rational weights that are \emph{eventually periodic}, and with
a positive value at a positive index of the periodic part, irrationality of
$\sum_a g(a)/(b^a-1)$ is proved here for every integer base $b\ge2$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{12397}{irrational_weightedErdosSeries_eventuallyPeriodic}{Lean};
Luca and Tachiya prove a broader periodic rational-coefficient theorem by
different methods~\cite{lucatachiya}, and no priority is claimed).  So the
weighted problem is settled on the eventually periodic class and open on
$\Aw$.  What fails for $\Aw$ is not positivity and not rationality of the
values: it is that no bounded amount of the weight determines the rest.
Section~\ref{sec:rank} makes that last sentence a theorem.

\section{The rank theorem}\label{sec:rank}

For $j\ge0$ and $0\le r<2^j$ write $\ph_{j,r}$ for the dyadic section
$n\mapsto\ph(2^jn+r)$, regarded as an element of the $\Q$-vector space of
sequences $\N\to\Q$.

\begin{theorem}[exact level rank]\label{res:rank}
For every $e\ge0$ the canonical level-$e$ family of dyadic totient sections is
linearly independent over $\Q$, and the dimension of its span is exactly
$2^e+1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[infinite dimensionality]\label{res:infdim}
The span of $\{\ph_{j,r}: j\ge0,\ 0\le r<2^j\}$ is not finite-dimensional
over $\Q$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent The independence is
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{900}{linearIndependent_canonicalTotientKernelFamily}{checked},
the exact dimension is
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{954}{finrank_canonicalTotientKernel_eq}{checked},
and the corollary is
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{1014}{not_finiteDimensional_span_fullTotientKernel}{checked}.
The corollary follows from the theorem in one step: were the full span
finite-dimensional of dimension $d$, the level-$d$ family would embed in it
and force $2^d+1\le d$.

The mechanism is a separated evaluation minor.  For each level one produces,
by the Chinese remainder theorem together with Dirichlet's theorem on primes
in arithmetic progressions, a set of evaluation points at which the section
matrix has a nonzero minor with the required separation
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{847}{exists_separatedMinorCertificate_totientAffineOddFamily}{Lean}).
Even residues reduce to odd-core channels, so the count $2^e+1$ rather than
$2^{e+1}$ is exact rather than an estimate.

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{res:rank} is a statement about $\ph$, not about $\Stot$: it is
true, and its proof is complete, whether or not $\Stot$ is irrational.  It is
included here because it is the sharpest available form of ``no finite amount
of the totient's dyadic structure determines the rest'', which is the property
the eventually periodic weight theorem consumes and $\Aw$ lacks.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The rationality-side consequence.}
Binary long division turns a hypothetical rational value into an integer carry
recurrence.  Transporting Theorem~\ref{res:rank} through that recurrence gives:
if $\Stot$ were rational, one positive-multiplier tempered integral carry would
have canonical carry-section span of dimension at least $2^e-1$ for
\emph{every} $e$
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientCarryKernelRigidity.lean}{284}{not_irrational_totientSeries_implies_unbounded_carryRank_unconditional}{Lean},
using the
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientCarryKernelRigidity.lean}{211}{finrank_canonicalCarryKernel_ge_of_linearIndependent}{rank transport}).
This is an anti-compression barrier, and we state its limit plainly: it rules
out every rationality proof that would exhibit a carry of \emph{bounded} rank,
and it is not an irrationality criterion, because no finite-rank upper bound
for every rationality-supplied carry is proved.

\section{The denominator bound, and what it is worth}\label{sec:denominator}

\begin{theorem}[denominator exclusion]\label{res:denominator}
If $\Stot=p/q$ with $p\in\Z$ and $q\ge1$, then
\[
  q>79\,639\,646\,646\,701\,375\,323\,355\,774\,875\,831\,053
   \approx7.96\times10^{34}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\noindent This is
\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18056}{tsum_totient_div_pow_two_ne_ratCast_of_den_le_79639646646701375323355774875831053}{checked}
as a single statement about $\Stot$.

We state its provenance rather than leaving a reader to find it.  The proof is
an exact Farey-window exclusion: the underlying Lean lemma is a gap check over
the Farey window $[1,240]$, and the argument is the elementary one --- a
rational with small denominator cannot lie in a sufficiently narrow interval
around a well-approximated real, and enough digits of $\Stot$ are computable
to make the interval narrow.  \emph{The method is standard, and we claim no
novelty for it.}  What the entry contributes is the explicit constant at this
scale, discharged inside a kernel-checked proof rather than by a script.  A
reader who regards an explicit Farey exclusion as routine is not in
disagreement with this note; Theorem~\ref{res:rank}, not
Theorem~\ref{res:denominator}, is where we would ask a sceptical reader to
look.

The bound transfers verbatim to the probabilistic form of the constant: with
$X,Y$ independent and $\Pr(X=n)=\Pr(Y=n)=2^{-n}$ one has
$\Stot=\tfrac12+\Pr(\gcd(X,Y)=1)$, so that probability has no rational
representation of denominator at most
$39\,819\,823\,323\,350\,687\,661\,677\,887\,437\,915\,526$.

\section{Two no-go theorems about methods}\label{sec:nogo}

Two negative results are unconditional theorems, and they are the kind of
statement whose scope is easy to overstate, so each is given with the exact
class it closes.

\begin{proposition}[fixed-precision transport]\label{res:fixedprec}
At every fixed positive precision, each finite compatible valuation-unit word
admits a prefix-locked centred completion.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
(\mword{Erdos249257/TropicalCurvatureCarry.lean}{137}{fixedPrecisionTropicalNoGo}{Lean}.)
Closes: arguments that would derive a contradiction from a local signature
read at a precision fixed in advance.  Does \emph{not} close: arguments whose
precision grows with the scale, or which use global arithmetic information
about $\ph$.

\begin{proposition}[factor ideals and finite-rank shift algebra]\label{res:lcm}
For every $t\ge3$ there is a nonzero synthetic affine carry whose forcing
letters lie in the principal ideal generated by $\ph(\lcm(1,\dots,t))$, which
matches every stated whole-ray anchor, stays within the diagonal bounds, and
whose state is a strict survivor; the same countermodel survives every finite
integer shift polynomial.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
(\mword{Erdos249257/LcmFactorIdealPulseObstruction.lean}{798}{lcm_factorIdeal_finiteRank_shiftAlgebra_not_sufficient}{Lean}.)
Closes: factor-ideal, whole-ray-anchor and finite-rank shift-algebra arguments
taken alone.  Does \emph{not} close: anything nonlinear in fresh divisor
channels.  The construction is synthetic --- it does not assert that its
forcing letters occur as actual totient differences --- and that limitation is
part of the statement, not a caveat attached to it.

\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}

The problem is open and this note does not narrow it.  Precisely:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Irrationality of $\Stot$. No proof is claimed, and
  Theorem~\ref{res:denominator} bounds a denominator without excluding one.
\item The certificate route reduces irrationality to an exact condition: a
  finite integer separation calculation, with a rigorous tail bound, occurring
  beyond every cutoff.  Finite instances are checked; the unbounded supply is
  not, and \emph{an equivalence is not progress}.  We flag this explicitly
  because the reduction is the part of this work most likely to be mistaken
  for a result.
\item The natural analytic target --- a cancellation estimate for the
  first-harmonic block sum attached to the doubling orbit of $(2^h-1)\Stot$
  --- is unproved, and is strictly stronger than irrationality rather than
  equivalent to it.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent A route we would now take seriously, and do not have: adapt the
Gowers-uniformity and quantitative-correlation method of
\cite{taoteravainen2025} from the $0/1$ weight $\mathbf 1_{\text{primes}}$ to
an unbounded weight.  Their argument uses the prime-dilation identity
$\omega(pn)=\omega(n)+1-\mathbf 1_{p\mid n}$, whose main increment is constant
and whose error is sparse.  The corresponding statement for $\ph$ has a
valuation-dependent defect and no such clean form, so the transfer is not
routine; but it is the first place a reader who wants to close \#249 should
look, and it did not exist when this development began.
