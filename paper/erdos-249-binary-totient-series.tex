\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Erd\H{o}s~\#249 asks whether $\Stot=\sum_{n\ge1}\ph(n)/2^n$ is irrational.  It
is open, nothing here closes it, and this note reports what a kernel-checked
attack does yield, each claim bounded by the exact proposition a proof checker
accepted --- the negative results included.

For $j\ge0$ and $0\le r<2^j$ let $\ph_{j,r}\colon n\mapsto\ph(2^jn+r)$.  Main
theorem: $\ph_{0,0}$, $\ph_{1,0}$ and the sections $\ph_{j,r}$ with $r$ odd
form a $\Q$-basis of the span of \emph{all} the $\ph_{j,r}$, so that span is
infinite-dimensional, its level-$e$ part has dimension exactly $2^e+1$, and the
only $\Q$-linear relations among dyadic sections of $\ph$ are those generated by
two elementary identities.

Second, $\Stot\neq a/q$ for every $a\in\Z$ and every
$q\le 79\,639\,646\,646\,701\,375\,323\,355\,774\,875\,831\,053$, a Farey
exclusion whose method is standard: we name it first, claim no novelty, and add
only that the constant is \emph{exactly optimal} for its window.  Third, the
Lambert weight $\Aw=\ph*\mu$ of $\Stot$ is unbounded and no fixed positive
integer clears its coordinates $\Aw(n)/n$; that is what separates \#249 from
three settled neighbours.  Every equivalence below is labelled one: an
equivalence is not progress.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Every item is a proposition the pinned Lean kernel accepted, or
(\ref{res:open}) the problem itself; the tag gives its status in the claim
registry's taxonomy, and each \emph{Checked} line links the declarations at
commit~\texttt{\commitshort}.  Nothing in the list is an irrationality
criterion for $\Stot$, and no combination of the items becomes one.
Throughout $\ph_{j,r}(n)=\ph(2^jn+r)$, for
$j\ge0$ and $0\le r<2^j$, is the $(j,r)$ \emph{dyadic section} of Euler's
totient, a vector in $\Q^{\N}$.  Two elementary identities relate them: the
\emph{reductions} $\ph_{j+1,0}=2^{j}\ph_{1,0}$
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{150}{totientKernel_zero_residue}{checked})
and $\ph_{j,\,2^{t+1}s}=2^{t}\ph_{j-t-1,\,s}$ for $s$ odd with $2^{t+1}s<2^j$
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{159}{totientKernel_even_residue_reduce}{checked}).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*.},ref=R\arabic*,leftmargin=2.4em,
  itemsep=0.2em,topsep=0.25em]

\item\label{res:basis} \stag{proved here}
\textbf{Basis theorem.}  The family
$\mathcal B=\{\ph_{0,0},\ph_{1,0}\}\cup\{\ph_{j,r}:j\ge1,\ r\ \text{odd},\
0<r<2^j\}$ is linearly independent over $\Q$ and spans the same $\Q$-subspace
of $\Q^{\N}$ as the set of \emph{all} dyadic sections
$\{\ph_{j,r}:j\ge0,\ 0\le r<2^j\}$; so $\mathcal B$ is a basis of that span,
which is therefore infinite-dimensional.  Since the reductions make every
remaining section a rational multiple of a member of $\mathcal B$, the
$\Q$-linear relations among the dyadic sections of $\ph$ are exactly those the
reductions generate --- a one-line consequence of the checked statements, not a
further one.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{1133}{linearIndependent_oddCoreTotientKernelFamily}{independence},
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{1248}{span_range_fullTotientKernel_eq_span_range_oddCore}{span equality},
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{1260}{totientDyadicSectionBasis}{basis}.}

\item\label{res:rank} \stag{proved here}
\textbf{Exact finite-level rank.}  For every $e\ge0$ the $2^e+1$ sections
$\ph_{0,0}$, $\ph_{1,0}$ and $\ph_{j,r}$ with $1\le j\le e$, $r$ odd,
$0<r<2^j$ are linearly independent over $\Q$, so their span has dimension
exactly $2^e+1=2+\sum_{j=1}^{e}2^{j-1}$: exact, not an estimate, because the
even residues are already dependent by the reductions.  \ref{res:basis}
follows by finite character of linear independence.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{900}{linearIndependent_canonicalTotientKernelFamily}{independence},
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{954}{finrank_canonicalTotientKernel_eq}{dimension}.}

\textbf{Denominator exclusion, sharp for its window.}  If $\Stot=a/q$ with
$a\in\Z$ and $q\ge1$ then
$q>79\,639\,646\,646\,701\,375\,323\,355\,774\,875\,831\,053\approx7.96\times10^{34}$.

the exact first denominator at which that window's certificate fails.  The

\item\label{res:carryrank} \stag{unconditional progress}
\textbf{Carry anti-compression.}  If $\Stot$ were rational then some tempered
integral carry orbit for the coefficients $\ph$, with positive multiplier,
would have for \emph{every} $e$ carry sections through level $e$ spanning a
space of dimension at least $2^e-1$.
\emph{Closes:} every rationality proof that would exhibit a carry of bounded
section rank.  \emph{Does not close:} anything else --- it is not an
irrationality criterion, because no finite-rank upper bound is proved for the
carries that rationality supplies.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/TotientCarryKernelRigidity.lean}{284}{not_irrational_totientSeries_implies_unbounded_carryRank_unconditional}{the barrier},

\textbf{The \#249 weight.}  $\Stot=\sum_{d\ge1}\Aw(d)/(2^d-1)$ with
$\Aw=\ph*\mu$; here $\Aw\ge0$, $\Aw(p)=p-2$, $\Aw(p^k)=\ph(p^k)-\ph(p^{k-1})$,
and $\Aw$ is unbounded.

\mword{Erdos249257/MersenneLambertLadder.lean}{321}{primWeight_not_bounded}{unboundedness}.}

\item\label{res:periodic} \stag{formalised here}
\textbf{Eventually periodic weights are settled.}  For every integer base
$b\ge2$, if $\gamma\colon\N\to\Q$ is eventually periodic, nonnegative and
positive at some positive index of its periodic part, then
$\sum_{a}\gamma(a)/(b^a-1)$ is irrational (Luca and Tachiya prove a broader
periodic theorem by other methods~\cite{lucatachiya}; no priority is claimed).
By \ref{res:weight} the weight $\Aw$ is unbounded, hence not eventually
periodic, so \#249 lies just outside this class.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{12483}{irrational_ratWeightSeries_eventuallyPeriodic}{the periodic theorem}.}

\item\label{res:primindex} \stag{proved here}
\textbf{No fixed index clears the normalised weight.}  For every integer
$D\ge1$ some $n\ge1$ has $D\cdot\Aw(n)/n\notin\Z$, and any $D$ clearing every
coordinate up to a horizon $N\ge4$ is divisible by an explicit two-tier
primorial ($4$ at $p=2$, $p^2$ when $p^2\le N$, else $p$).
\emph{Closes:} every argument clearing the coordinates $\Aw(n)/n$ by a single
positive integer valid at all $n$.  \emph{Does not close:} arguments whose
index grows with $n$, or which never clear the coordinates.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/PrimitiveDeterminantLift.lean}{169}{no_fixed_integral_primitive_euler_index}{no fixed index},
\mword{Erdos249257/PrimitiveDeterminantLift.lean}{148}{twoTierPrimorial_dvd_of_integral_jet}{the primorial divisibility}.}

\item\label{res:reduction} \stag{conditional reduction}
\textbf{Two exact reformulations, offered as nothing more.}  Irrationality of
$\Stot$ is equivalent to a pointwise finite-certificate condition, and follows
from a supply of gap certificates --- one window $(N,K)$ per denominator $q$
whose totient carry residue avoids a band of width $q(N{+}K{+}2)$ out of $2^K$.
Neither hypothesis is proved for one unbounded family.  Both are restatements
of \#249 in other coordinates, and are the part of this work most likely to be
mistaken for a result.
{\footnotesize\emph{Checked:}
\mword{Erdos249257/LcmConeFlatness.lean}{399}{irrational_totient_series_iff_pointwise_certificates}{the equivalence},
\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{15697}{irrational_tsum_totient_div_pow_two_of_gap_certificate_supply}{the supply implication}.}

\item\label{res:open} \stag{open}
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s~\#249.}  Whether $\Stot$ is irrational.  Not proved here.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Where \#249 sits}\label{sec:family}

If $f=g*\mathbf 1$, that is $f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}g(d)$, then interchanging the
two sums gives
\[
  \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{f(n)}{2^n}
  \;=\;\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{g(d)}{2^d-1}.
  \tag{1}\label{eq:lambert}
\]
Four classical coefficient sequences have this shape.  The table records the
constant, the weight $g=f*\mu$, and its status.  Decimals were recomputed to
$50$ places for this note and are truncated, not rounded.

  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\linewidth}
  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.22\linewidth}
  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.37\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
$f$ & $\sum f(n)/2^n$ & weight $g=f*\mu$ & status \\
\midrule
$\tau$ & $1.60669515\ldots=\EB$ & $g\equiv1$ & Irrational (Erd\H{o}s 1948);
  formalised here in the Lambert form $\sum_{d\ge1}(2^d-1)^{-1}$
  (\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{8007}{irrational_erdosBorwein_series}{checked}),
  equal to $\sum\tau(n)/2^n$ by~\eqref{eq:lambert} \\

  Irrational (\emph{ibid.}, remark) \\

\end{tabular}

\noindent The first three weights are bounded, taking only the values $0$
and~$1$ (for $\tau$ the weight is the constant $1$, so only the value~$1$).
The fourth is unbounded, by \ref{res:weight}.  That difference is the one
that matters for the route in \ref{res:periodic}, and we do not claim it is
the only difference: $\Aw$ also vanishes on $n\equiv2\pmod4$, while
$\mathbf 1_{\text{primes}}$ is bounded but no more eventually periodic than
$\Aw$ is, so the settled $\omega$ and $\Omega$ cases are not instances of
\ref{res:periodic} either.  They were settled by a different method, discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

The difficulty of \#249 does not live in the Lambert transform.  The
neighbouring rungs are rational and exactly computable ---
$\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)=\tfrac12$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18106}{tsum_moebius_div_two_pow_sub_one_eq_half}{checked})
and $\sum_{d\ge1}\ph(d)/(2^d-1)=2$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18101}{tsum_totient_div_two_pow_sub_one_eq_two}{checked})
--- and the signed squared-Mersenne lens
$\Stot=\tfrac12+\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)^2$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18126}{totient_series_eq_half_add_moebius_mersenne_square}{checked})
recomputes $\Stot$ to all $50$ places.  The difficulty lives on the
power-series side, and \ref{res:basis} is a statement about exactly that side.

\section{The basis theorem}\label{sec:rank}

The producer for \ref{res:rank} is a separated evaluation minor.  For each
level, the Chinese remainder theorem together with Dirichlet's theorem on
primes in arithmetic progressions supplies a set of evaluation points at which
the section matrix has a nonzero minor with the required two-adic separation:
one affine channel is made prime, and every other channel receives a fresh
prime divisor congruent to $1$ modulo a large power of two, so the columns
carry distinct exact powers of two and the determinant cannot vanish
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{847}{exists_separatedMinorCertificate_totientAffineOddFamily}{checked}).
Even residues never enter, because the second reduction has already carried
them to odd-core channels
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientMahlerDefect.lean}{159}{totientKernel_even_residue_reduce}{checked}).

\ref{res:basis} then needs only two further steps, and both are short.  Linear
independence is a property of finite subfamilies, so the level-$e$ producer,
applied with $e$ above the largest level occurring in a given finite subset,
already gives independence of the whole infinite family $\mathcal B$.  And
the two reductions carry every remaining section onto a rational multiple of a
member of $\mathcal B$, so the spans agree.  That the
assembly is short is the point: the level-$e$ theorem was always the whole
content, and stating its consequence as infinite-dimensionality understated
it.  The sharp form is a basis.

\begin{remark}
\ref{res:basis} and \ref{res:rank} are statements about $\ph$, not about
$\Stot$; they are true, and their proofs are complete, whether or not $\Stot$
is irrational.  They are here because they are the exact form of ``no finite
amount of the totient's dyadic structure determines the rest'' --- the
property that \ref{res:periodic} consumes for an eventually periodic weight
and that $\Aw$ lacks.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The rationality side.}
Binary long division turns a hypothetical rational value into an integer carry
recurrence: for coefficients $c(n)\le n$, and in particular for $c=\ph$, the
series $\sum c(n)/2^n$ is rational exactly when a tempered integer carry orbit
exists
(\mword{Erdos249257/GenericTailOrbitRigidity.lean}{425}{binaryCoeffSeries_rational_iff_exists_temperedBinaryOrbit}{checked}).
Transporting \ref{res:rank} through that recurrence gives \ref{res:carryrank}.
The scope line there is worth repeating: this is an anti-compression barrier,
and the complementary finite-rank upper bound, which is what would make it a
proof, is not available.

\section{The denominator bound}\label{sec:denominator}

\ref{res:denominator} is a Farey exclusion, and we say so before saying
anything else about it.  The argument is the classical mediant one.  The Lean
lemma commits the first $240$ binary digits of the shifted series as a single
residue over the totient carry window $(N,K)=(1,240)$; a rational $a/q$ can
equal $\Stot$ only if it falls into a resulting bad interval of width
$243/2^{240}$; that interval is bracketed by two explicit unimodular fractions
$a_1/b<c_1/d$ with $bc_1-a_1d=1$; and the mediant lemma
(\mword{Erdos249257/GapFareyBound.lean}{51}{farey_gap}{checked}) forces any
rational strictly between unimodular neighbours to have denominator at least
$b+d$.  The excluded range is therefore $q\le b+d-1$, which is the printed
constant
(\mword{Erdos249257/GapFareyBound.lean}{176}{gap_check_window_1_240_le_79639646646701375323355774875831053}{checked}),
and the transport to the series is a tail estimate
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{15658}{tsum_totient_div_pow_two_ne_int_div_of_totient_gap_certificate}{checked}).
\emph{The method is standard, and we claim no novelty for it.}

Two things should be read off correctly.  First, the scale.  A window of $K$
computed binary digits yields a mediant bound of order $2^{K/2}$: the same
pipeline gives $2.49\times10^{17}$ at $K=120$
(\mword{Erdos249257/GapFareyBound.lean}{88}{gap_check_window_1_120_le_248672326362367909}{checked})
and $7.96\times10^{34}$ at $K=240$.  The constant is a function of how many
digits were committed, not a measure of how much the problem has moved.
Second, the sharpness.  Within its window the bound cannot be improved at all:
$b+d$ itself fails the certificate, so $b+d-1$ is exactly the last denominator
excluded (\ref{res:denominator}).  A reader who regards an explicit Farey
exclusion as routine is not in disagreement with this note.
\ref{res:basis}, not \ref{res:denominator}, is where we would ask a sceptical
reader to look.

The bound transfers to the coprimality form of the constant with the constant
halved.  Writing the visible-coprime-pair series as
$\sum_{\gcd(m,n)=1,\ m,n\ge1}2^{-(m+n)}$, one has
$\Stot=\tfrac12+\sum_{\gcd(m,n)=1}2^{-(m+n)}$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18229}{totient_series_eq_half_add_visible_coprime_pairs}{checked}),
so that series has no representation $a/d$ with
$d\le39\,819\,823\,323\,350\,687\,661\,677\,887\,437\,915\,526$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CertificateKernel.lean}{18244}{tsum_visible_coprime_pairs_ne_int_div_of_den_le_39819823323350687661677887437915526}{checked}),
that number being $(q_0-1)/2$ for the constant $q_0$ of \ref{res:denominator}.
This series is the probability that two independent geometric fair-coin
waiting times are coprime; the checked statements are the series identity and
the series exclusion, and the probabilistic reading is a gloss on them rather
than a formalised measure-theoretic statement.

\section{Barriers, and two we do not state as theorems}\label{sec:nogo}

\ref{res:primindex} is the one method barrier this note states, and its scope
lines are given with it in Section~\ref{sec:results}.  It is worth a sentence
of mechanism, because the mechanism is the whole of it: take a prime
$p>\max(D,2)$; then $\Aw(p)=p-2$ by \ref{res:weight}, and $p\nmid D(p-2)$, so
$D\cdot\Aw(p)/p\notin\Z$.  Being one line is a virtue here.  The statement is
about the weight the abstract already names, it needs no vocabulary this note
has not defined, and it survives a hostile reading.

Two further no-go results in the formal corpus appeared as propositions in an
earlier version of this note, and are demoted rather than dropped, because the
reason is more useful to a reader than the propositions were.

A fixed-precision transport result
(\mword{Erdos249257/TropicalCurvatureCarry.lean}{137}{fixedPrecisionTropicalNoGo}{checked})
says that at every fixed positive precision, each finite compatible
valuation-unit word admits a prefix-locked centred completion.  Its statement
quantifies over abstract pairs of a two-adic valuation and an odd unit; it
mentions neither $\ph$ nor $\Stot$, its content is that a congruence class
meets any interval of the corresponding length, and it is a restatement of a
lemma printed immediately above it.  The class of arguments it closes ---
deriving a contradiction from a local signature read at a precision fixed in
advance, along a finite word --- is not one anybody has proposed for \#249.

A factor-ideal result
(\mword{Erdos249257/LcmFactorIdealPulseObstruction.lean}{798}{lcm_factorIdeal_finiteRank_shiftAlgebra_not_sufficient}{checked},
with a sparse-anchor companion at
\mword{Erdos249257/LcmFactorIdealPulseObstruction.lean}{866}{lcm_factorIdeal_sparseAnchor_not_sufficient}{checked})
exhibits, for each $t\ge3$ with $H=\lcm(1,\dots,t)$, a nonzero integer carry
whose forcing letters lie in the ideal generated by $\ph(H)$, which reproduces
the true totient differences at $t-2$ prescribed indices, and which every
finite integer shift polynomial carries to a pair with the same coboundary
form, the same ideal memberships and an $\ell^1$-weight bound.  It does close
that hypothesis class.  Two limits.  The witness is a spike --- its state is
$-\ph(H)$ at $t-2$ points and zero elsewhere, so it stays uniformly bounded
while the strip bounds it respects grow, and the construction is cheap for
that reason.  And the shifted pairs are not asserted to be nonzero, so an
argument that additionally demanded non-vanishing after the shift is not
closed.  An earlier draft of this note also
attributed to it a whole-ray anchor condition, a diagonal-bound condition and
a strict-survivor condition that the cited theorem does not contain, and
described it as ``surviving'' the shift algebra when the checked conclusion
carries no non-vanishing clause.  Correcting that is the reason it is here at
all.

\section{Three problem-owned programmes}\label{sec:programmes}

The modules below are problem-owned rather than part of the reviewed corpus.
Each states its unproved hypothesis explicitly; none advances \#249, and we
list them because a reader deciding whether to spend time here should see the
sufficient conditions in the exact form Lean checks them.

\paragraph{Strict prime-orbit escape.}
The cleanest of the three, because it moves a threshold.  The producer
\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{18}{DTWNaturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{asks}
for cofinally many primes $p$ at which the first tail-orbit exponential has
real part strictly below $9/10$.  Granting it,
\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{27}{naturalPivotPointEscape_of_naturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{a positive adaptive truncation budget}
follows, and then
\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{66}{irrational_totient_series_of_naturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{$S$ is irrational}.
The point of interest is the constant: the legacy route required a $4/5$ gap,
this one requires only $9/10$, so the sufficient condition is strictly weaker
while the conclusion is unchanged.  Weakening a hypothesis you still cannot
verify is progress on the \emph{statement}, not on the problem, and the
producer remains unproved.

\paragraph{Finite Euler-sieve algebra.}
Elementary identities behind the squared-M\"obius approximation to the totient
generating function; no transcendence input is claimed.  The local Euler
factors are exact squares at
\lword{Erdos249/FiniteEulerSieve.lean}{28}{muSqEulerFactor_one}{$s=1$}
and
\lword{Erdos249/FiniteEulerSieve.lean}{36}{muSqEulerFactor_two}{$s=2$},
and on the divisor-sum prime-power row
\lword{Erdos249/FiniteEulerSieve.lean}{44}{sigmaPrimePow_firstDiff}{the first difference}
is $p-1$ while
\lword{Erdos249/FiniteEulerSieve.lean}{51}{sigmaPrimePow_secondDiff}{the second difference}
is $p^{e+1}(p-1)$.  The second difference is the content: applying
$\mu\ast\mu$ converts the divisor-sum row into the prime-power totient row
exactly, at every finite stage.  These are one-line facts; they are recorded
because the approximation they license is otherwise easy to assert without
checking.

\paragraph{Prime-ray cyclotomic curvature.}
A producer/consumer interface with two named obligations.  Its curvature
functional is
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{15}{checkerboard}{the checkerboard}
on a two-anchor rectangle, which
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{19}{checkerboard_separable}{annihilates every separable background}
$u(x)+v(y)$ and is
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{25}{checkerboard_unique}{the unique such $2\times2$ annihilator up to scale}.
That uniqueness is worth stating: the operator is not a convenient choice
among many, it is the only one, which is what makes the resulting obstruction
coordinate-free.  On top of it sit
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{38}{fourPoint_layer_identity}{a squarefree divisor-layer cancellation}
and
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{55}{residueClass_step_has_centred_completion}{a centred-completion step for residue classes modulo an even modulus}.
The two obligations are
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{88}{PrimeRayLayerSupply}{eventual clean cyclotomic layers on a prime ray}
and
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{95}{BoundedDegreeOrderConsumer}{an exact-order witness at every rational prime divisor of a layer};
together they give
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{102}{FinitePrimeSupportEscape}{escape from every prescribed finite prime support}.
Neither obligation is proved here, and polynomial resultant realisability and
Archimedean growth remain upstream of both.

\section{What we need from a mathematician}\label{sec:ask}

Three obligations carry the whole conditional part of this work.  Each is a
statement about one explicitly defined sequence, each is stated below from
definitions given here, and none needs the rest of this note --- or any Lean
--- to be understood, proved or refuted.  They are written in the form in
which we would want an answer.

Two remarks apply to all three.  A negative answer is worth exactly as much to
us as a positive one: if a route provably cannot supply what it is asked for,
we will say so here and drop the route rather than leave it listed as live.
And none of the three asks anyone to grant anything about $\Stot$; each is an
ordinary question about a defined object, answerable, refutable or
recognisable from the literature on its own terms.

\paragraph{Ask 1.  Certificates beyond every cutoff, on the $\lcm$ diagonal.}
Write $H_t=\lcm(1,\dots,t)$
(\mword{Erdos249257/CarrySurvivorExtinction.lean}{515}{periodLcm}{definition})
and let $R_N=\sum_{m\ge1}\ph(N+m)/2^{m}$ be the fractional layer of
$2^N\Stot$
(\mword{Erdos249257/TotientTailPeriodKiller.lean}{57}{totientTail}{definition}).
\emph{Wanted:} for every $t_0$ there is a $t\ge t_0$ with
$R_{2H_t}-R_{H_t}\notin\Z$.  That is the hypothesis on the right of
\mword{Erdos249257/LcmConeFlatness.lean}{426}{irrational_totient_series_iff_lcm_diagonal_certificate_supply}{the diagonal normal form},
in the finite-certificate coordinates the two forms share
(\mword{Erdos249257/LcmConeFlatness.lean}{439}{periodLcm_diagonal_kill_iff_tail_diff_notMem_int}{checked}).

\emph{Already settled, so that nobody repeats it.}  The normal form is an
equivalence, not a sufficient condition: $\Stot$ is irrational if and only if
that supply exists.  Nothing short of a cofinal statement is therefore of use,
and no finite band approximates one.  The converse direction is sharper than
it looks --- one pair $h>0$, $N$ with $R_{N+h}-R_N\in\Z$ already makes
$\Stot$ rational
(\mword{Erdos249257/LcmConeFlatness.lean}{357}{not_irrational_of_tail_diff_mem_int}{checked}).
The certificate is decidable at each $t$, and has been kernel-checked to fire
at the $28$ scales
(\mword{Erdos249257/DiagonalPincerCertificatesT64.lean}{1933}{diagonalPincerCertificateScalesThroughT64}{listed})
lying between $t=1$ and $t=64$
(\mword{Erdos249257/DiagonalPincerCertificatesT64.lean}{1967}{certifiedKill_diagonal_all_imported_through_t64}{checked}).
Lengthening that list is not progress towards the ask, and is not offered as
such: a band bounded at $t=64$ contributes nothing at $t=65$.

\emph{Most useful first.}  (i) Any argument giving $R_{2H_t}-R_{H_t}\notin\Z$
for infinitely many $t$, with no uniformity or rate required.  (ii) A reason
the $\lcm$ diagonal is the wrong ray to ask this of --- for instance that
$R_{2H_t}-R_{H_t}$ is beyond the reach of present methods for structural
reasons, not merely unproved.  (iii) A single $t$ with $R_{2H_t}-R_{H_t}\in\Z$.
A construction or a proof (i) helps most; (ii) is next and would be acted on.

\emph{If answered.}  (i) closes \#249, since the normal form is an
equivalence and the deduction is already checked.  (ii) retires the diagonal
presentation, and with it Ask~1, without touching the problem.  (iii) settles
\#249 in the opposite direction, which would be a larger result than anything
asked for here.

\paragraph{Ask 2.  One cofinal prime with first-harmonic real part below
$9/10$.}
\emph{Wanted:} for every $h\ge1$ and every $N_0$, a prime
$p\ge\max(N_0+h+1,\,h+5)$ with
\[
  \cos\bigl(2\pi\,(R_{p-1}-R_{p-h-1})\bigr)<\tfrac{9}{10},
\]
equivalently with $R_{p-1}-R_{p-h-1}$ further than
$\arccos(9/10)/2\pi=0.07178\ldots$ from the nearest integer.  Written with
$N=p-h-1$, this is
\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{18}{DTWNaturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{the hypothesis}
verbatim: the real part of the first additive character of $R_{N+h}-R_N$,
below $9/10$ for cofinally many primes.

\emph{Already settled.}  The quantity is not an artefact of the truncation:
integer prefixes cancel under the character, so this is exactly the first
additive character of $2^{N}(2^{h}-1)\Stot$
(\mword{Erdos249257/PivotAntiReconstruction.lean}{66}{tailOrbitFirstExp_eq_scaledTotientSeriesFirstExp}{checked}),
that is, of one orbit of the doubling map.  Granting the gap, the adaptive
truncation budget
(\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{27}{naturalPivotPointEscape_of_naturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{checked})
and then irrationality
(\lword{Erdos249/TotientStrictPrimeEscape.lean}{66}{irrational_totient_series_of_naturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap}{checked})
follow with no further input.  The threshold has already been moved: the
legacy route consumed a $4/5$ gap and this one consumes $9/10$, so the
hypothesis is strictly weaker than the one it replaced.  We have no proof of
it at either threshold.

\emph{Most useful first.}  (i) A reference.  This is a one-sided escape
statement for a single explicit real orbit sampled along the primes, and an
analytic number theorist may recognise it as standard, as a known consequence
of an existing estimate, or as a known hard case; any of the three would be
decisive for how we spend time.  (ii) A proof or proof sketch.  (iii) A reason
the statement is out of reach for orbits of this kind.  (i) helps most,
because the cost of the wrong answer here is measured in months.

\emph{If answered.}  (i) or (ii) closes \#249 through the checked endpoint
above.  (iii) closes only this branch: the gap is sufficient for
irrationality and nothing here shows it necessary, so its failure would leave
the problem exactly where it is.  This is the ask we consider most likely to
be answerable by somebody who has not read the rest of this note.

\paragraph{Ask 3.  Bounded multiplicative order along a prime ray.}
Fix integers $m,d\ge1$ and a layer sequence $C\colon\N\to\N$.  Two statements
are wanted, of which the second is the substantial one.
(a) There is a $Q_0$ with $C(mq)>1$ and $\gcd(C(mq),mq)=1$ for every prime
$q\ge Q_0$
(\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{88}{PrimeRayLayerSupply}{the supply}).
(b) Whenever $q$ and $p$ are prime and $p\mid C(mq)$, some $k$ with
$1\le k\le d$ has $mq\mid p^{k}-1$
(\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{95}{BoundedDegreeOrderConsumer}{the consumer}).
Statement (b) is a primitive-divisor question in classical shape: it asks that
every prime divisor of a layer have multiplicative order bounded by $d$
relative to the ray index $mq$.

\emph{Already settled.}  The curvature coordinates are forced rather than
chosen --- the checkerboard is the only $2\times2$ joint annihilator up to
scale
(\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{25}{checkerboard_unique}{checked}) ---
and the divisor-layer cancellation
(\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{38}{fourPoint_layer_identity}{checked})
and the affine recentring step
(\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{55}{residueClass_step_has_centred_completion}{checked})
are proved from their stated hypotheses.  What is \emph{not} there should be
said plainly: (a), (b) and the intended consequence
\lword{Erdos249/PrimeRayCyclotomicCurvature.lean}{102}{FinitePrimeSupportEscape}{finite-prime-support escape}
are all recorded as definitions, and the module proves neither obligation.
One line, offered as prose and not as a checked statement: (b) alone already
forces the escape by size, since $p\mid C(mq)$ gives $mq\le p^{d}-1$, so for a
finite set $S$ of primes no member of $S$ divides $C(mq)$ once
$q>\max_{p\in S}p^{d}/m$.  The role of (a) is then to keep the conclusion from
being vacuous, since $C(mq)=1$ would satisfy it trivially.

\emph{Most useful first.}  (i) A reference: whether (b) is known, in either
direction, for cyclic resultants of an integer polynomial, which is the
intended source of $C$.  (ii) A counterexample family for which (b) fails.
(iii) A construction of a layer sequence satisfying both.  A reference helps
most, because we do not know whether we are asking something classical or
something false.

\emph{If answered.}  This closes one branch and not the problem.  Escape from
every prescribed finite prime support is not irrationality of $\Stot$:
realising $C$ by cyclic resultants at all, and the Archimedean growth
estimate, are both upstream, both unproved, and no route from escape to \#249
is claimed here.  An answer tells us whether to keep the branch; it does not
move \#249.

\paragraph{Not an ask: the denominator bound.}
The constant $7.96\times10^{34}$ of \ref{res:denominator} is not an open item,
and help with it is not wanted.  It is the classical Farey mediant bound
applied to a window with a free parameter $K$, it has order $2^{K/2}$, and
raising it needs only more committed binary digits --- a computation, not an
idea.  A reader whose first instinct is to improve that number would be
spending effort at the one point in this note where the method is completely
understood and the return is nil.

\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}

The problem is open and this note does not narrow it.  Precisely:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Irrationality of $\Stot$ (\ref{res:open}).  No proof is claimed.
\item The certificate routes of \ref{res:reduction} convert irrationality into
  the existence of an unbounded supply of finite integer certificates.  Finite
  instances are checked --- \ref{res:denominator} is one, at $K=240$ --- and
  the unbounded supply is not.  \emph{An equivalence is not progress.}
\item The natural analytic target --- a cancellation estimate for the
  first-harmonic block sum attached to the doubling orbit of
  $(2^h-1)\Stot$ --- is unproved, and is strictly stronger than irrationality
  rather than equivalent to it.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent A route we would now take seriously, and do not have: adapt the
Gowers-uniformity and quantitative-correlation method
of~\cite{taoteravainen2025} from the bounded weight
$\mathbf 1_{\text{primes}}$ to an unbounded weight.  Their argument uses the
prime-dilation identity
$\omega(pn)=\omega(n)+1-\mathbf 1_{p\mid n}$, whose main increment is constant
and whose error is sparse.  For $\ph$ the corresponding correction is
valuation-dependent, and the formal corpus makes the difficulty precise rather
than impressionistic: on any prescribed finite horizon the correction
\emph{can} be suppressed, by a bounded simultaneous square-CRT base
(\mword{Erdos249257/SquareCRTCube.lean}{228}{exists_squareCRT_clean_horizon}{checked}),
but suppression alone forces nothing, since a clean block can vanish
(\mword{Erdos249257/SquareCRTCube.lean}{285}{squareCRT_clean_block_can_vanish}{checked})
and can equally be nonzero
(\mword{Erdos249257/SquareCRTCube.lean}{303}{squareCRT_clean_block_can_be_nonzero}{checked}).
So the transfer is not blocked by an obvious obstruction and is not routine
either.  It is the first place a reader who wants to close \#249 should look,
and it did not exist when this development began.
