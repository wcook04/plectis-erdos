\section{The problem, and what is actually known}
\label{sec:257-problem}

\subsection{The object and the two exact statements}

For an integer $n \ge 1$ set $x_n := 1/(2^n-1)$, and for $A \subseteq \Npos$ write
$x_A := \sum_{n \in A} x_n$; the sum always converges, since $x_n = O(2^{-n})$. Erd\H{o}s's
Problem \#257 asks a single universally quantified question about this family.

\begin{defn}[Universal \#257, written (U)]
\label{defn:U}
Is $x_A = \sum_{n \in A} 1/(2^n-1)$ irrational for \emph{every} infinite $A \subseteq \Npos$?
\textbf{OPEN.} \ev{Open}
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[The half-value question, written (H)]
\label{defn:H}
Let the \emph{Mersenne achievement set} be
$\mathcal{A} := \{\, y \in \R : \exists A \subseteq \N,\ 0 \notin A,\ y = \sum_{n \in A} x_n \,\}$
(\lean{mersenneAchievementSet}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:571}). Is $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$?
\textbf{OPEN.} \ev{Open}
\end{defn}

The two are joined by one elementary and completely one-sided implication. Every $2^n-1$ is odd,
so every \emph{finite} subset sum of $\{x_n\}$ has odd reduced denominator and cannot equal $1/2$
(\lean{positiveMersenneSupportValue\_coe\_finset\_ne\_half}{HalfCutLocator.lean:243}). Hence a
witness to $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$ is necessarily an infinite $A$ with $x_A = 1/2$ rational, which
refutes (U) outright. Conversely, (U) implies $1/2 \notin \mathcal{A}$ as one instance among
uncountably many, and a proof of $1/2 \notin \mathcal{A}$ leaves (U) entirely open. So (H) is the
maximally symmetric candidate counterexample to (U), not a restatement of it; the asymmetry is
load-bearing and recurs below as Barrier~\ref{bar:asym}. \ev{Math}

Nothing in this document decides (U) or (H). What it does contain is (i) the unconditional record,
stated with hypotheses and evidence bands, and (ii) a characterisation of \emph{why} the recorded
attacks fail --- assembled so that the failures function as measurements of one obstruction rather
than as a list of disappointments.

Two one-sided rigidity results constrain a hypothetical rational-valued support, both only from the

companion \lean{shifted\_state\_unbounded\_of\_infinite\_support}{RationalSupportCarrySkeleton.lean:2327}
is essentially tight and is best read as a restatement rather than a constraint.

\begin{rem}[What is and is not covered in the universal direction]
The pinned Lean corpus contains no theorem for $A=$ the primes, but this is a
formalisation boundary, not a mathematical open case: Tao and
Ter\"av\"ainen prove the base-$2$ prime-support value irrational by first
identifying it with $\sum_{n\ge1}\omega(n)2^{-n}$.  Duverney and Tachiya
likewise settle squarefree support at every power-of-two base.  Neither
result is formalised here.  The corpus still has no general theorem for an
arbitrary positive-density nonperiodic or divisor-dense support, and the
universal statement remains open.
\end{rem}

\subsection{Divisibility-weighted supports: why averaging twice works}
\label{sec:257-weighted-returns}

The following ordinary proof extends the reciprocal-summable theorem to
explicit supports whose reciprocal sum diverges. Its full source, including
the other analytic support criteria, is the
\href{https://github.com/wcook04/plectis-lean-erdos249-257/blob/e8458cdce502436d27af8a77d1e4d6742d3bcb69/docs/research/erdos257-eight-return-proofs.md}{analytic proof supplement}.

\begin{thm}[Divisibility-weighted irrationality]\label{thm:257-weighted}
Let $P$ be a finite nonempty set of primes and set
$h(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}$. For an integer $b\ge2$ and an infinite
$A\subseteq\Npos$, suppose
\[
 W=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{h(a)}{a(b^{h(a)}-1)}<\infty.
\]
Then $S_b(A)=\sum_{a\in A}(b^a-1)^{-1}$ is irrational. The condition at
$b=2$ gives this conclusion at every integer base $b\ge2$ and for every
infinite subset of $A$. \ev{Math}
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 \Delta(m)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{b^{m\bmod a}-1}{b^a-1}.
\]
For fixed $m$ the sum converges. It is positive for $m>0$, because $A$
contains an $a>m$. Division of $m$ by each $a$ gives the finite integer
identity
\[
 \Delta(m)=(b^m-1)S_b(A)
 -\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a\le m}}
       \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor m/a\rfloor}b^{m-ia}.
\]
Thus $S_b(A)=u/v$, $v>0$, would force $\Delta(m)\ge1/v$ for every
positive $m$. We construct arbitrarily small positive displacements.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Choose a finite $F\subset A$ whose complementary
weighted mass is less than $\varepsilon$, and put $L=\operatorname{lcm}F$
(with $L=1$ if $F$ is empty). Let $p_* =\max P$, $r=|P|$, and, for large
$H$, set
\[
 Q=L\prod_{p\in P}p^{\lfloor\log_p H\rfloor},\qquad
 G=\lfloor H/p_*\rfloor.
\]
Then $Q\le LH^r$ and every atom in $F$ has zero displacement on $Q\N$.
If $h(a)\le H$, then $h(a)\mid Q$; if $h(a)>H$, then $(a,Q)\ge G$.
For the latter assertion, either all prime-power factors of $h(a)$ are
at most $H$, so $h(a)\mid Q$, or one such factor exceeds $H$ and the
corresponding factor of $Q$ is greater than $H/p_*$.

Put $g=(a,Q)$. One orbit of $Qm\bmod a$ visits
$0,g,\ldots,a-g$, and the sum of its uncentred atoms is $(b^g-1)^{-1}$.
Complete orbits and one partial orbit therefore give
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{m=1}^T\frac{b^{Qm\bmod a}-1}{b^a-1}
 \le \frac{g}{a(b^g-1)}+\frac1{T(b^g-1)}.
\]
The function $g/(b^g-1)$ decreases for positive integral $g$. Splitting
at $a=QT$ and $h(a)=H$ consequently gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal A(Q,T):=\frac1T\sum_{m=1}^T\Delta(Qm)
 \le {}&\varepsilon+
 \frac1T\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a\le QT\\h(a)\le H}}
               \frac1{b^{h(a)}-1}\\
 &+\frac{G(1+\log(QT))+Q}{b^G-1}+\frac4T.
\end{align*}
Here the harmonic bound handles the large-$h$ terms. For $a>QT$ there is
no wrap: its atom is at most $2\,2^{Qm-a}$; summing first over $a$, then
over $m$, gives the final $4/T$.

The remaining incomplete-period sum need not have finite total mass.
Instead average over $T=2^j$, $M\le j<2M$. For each fixed $a$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{M\le j<2M\\a\le Q2^j}}2^{-j}\le\frac{2Q}{a}.
\]
This is a geometric tail starting at the first admissible $j$. Multiplying
by $(b^{h(a)}-1)^{-1}$ and interchanging the two finite sums charges this
error at most $2QW$, rather than $M$ separate copies of a divergent sum.
Hence
\[
 \frac1M\sum_{j=M}^{2M-1}\mathcal A(Q,2^j)
 \le\varepsilon+\frac{2QW}{M}
 +\frac{G(1+\log Q+2M\log2)+Q}{b^G-1}+4\,2^{-M}.
\]
Choose $M=\lfloor b^{G/2}\rfloor$. Since $Q\le LH^r$ and
$G\sim H/p_*$, every term after $\varepsilon$ tends to zero as
$H\to\infty$. A finite average below $2\varepsilon$ contains a
displacement below $2\varepsilon$. This contradicts the rational lattice
gap. Heredity follows from positivity of the weights, and the all-base
assertion follows from $b^{h(a)}-1\ge2^{h(a)}-1$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A concrete support beyond reciprocal summability.}
For $k\ge0$ put $B_k=2^{\,2^{\,2^k-k}}$ and
$A^*=\{a\ge1:a\le B_{v_2(a)}\}$. In the layer $v_2(a)=k$, writing
$a=2^ku$ with $u$ odd gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{a\le B_k\\v_2(a)=k}}
 \frac{2^k}{a(2^{2^k}-1)}
 \le\frac{1+\log B_k}{2^{2^k}-1}
 \le 2^{1-2^k}+2(\log2)2^{-k}.
\]
These bounds sum to a finite number. In contrast, the reciprocal mass of
the same layer is at least
$(\log B_k-\log(4\cdot2^k))/2^{k+1}$ for large $k$, which tends to
infinity. Every infinite subset of $A^*$ therefore has irrational $S_b$
at every integer base. The supplement also shows that consecutive elements
of $A^*$ have gaps $O(\log\log a)$.

\paragraph{Other support criteria sharing the small-shift endgame.}
If $H_A(x)=\sum_{a\in A,\,a\le x}1/a=o(\log^*x)$, then every
infinite subset of $A$ has irrational $S_b$ at every integer base. A second
criterion uses finite frames $F_j\subseteq\Npos$, their incidences
$f_F(n)=\#\{a\in F:a\mid n\}$, exponents $0<\alpha_j\le1$, and
nonnegative divisor majorants
$f_{F_j}(n)^{\alpha_j}\le\sum_{d\mid n}c_{j,d}$. With
$C_j=\sum_{d\ge1}c_{j,d}/d$ and $B_j=2^{\alpha_j}$, it suffices that
\[
 \sum_j C_j2^{j\alpha_j}\frac{B_j}{(B_j-1)^2}<\infty.
\]
Every infinite subset of $\bigcup_jF_j$ then has irrational $S_b$ at all
integer bases. Fixed-exponent summable-cost covers fit this criterion after
regrouping. The short paper and supplement prove these statements, including
the domination required before exchanging infinite sums and averages.
The analytic statements in this subsection are ordinary proofs; the pinned
formal-source table above records their separately verified predecessors.

\begin{rem}[Literature, exactly]
$E$ is irrational (Erd\H{o}s 1948). Zudilin's irrationality measure is
$\mu(E) \le 2.42343562\ldots$ (\emph{Math.\ Notes} 72 (2002) 858--862; the erratum, \emph{Acta
Arith.} 111 (2004) 153--164, gives $2.46497868\ldots$). Transcendence of $E$ is unknown
(Duverney--Tachiya, \emph{Forum Math.} 31 (2019) 1557--1566), and the Erd\H{o}s--Graham conjecture
that every such subset sum is irrational --- i.e.\ (U) --- is itself open. For binary digit runs of
$E$ the only rigorous result is a \emph{lower} bound: Erd\H{o}s constructs a $0$-run of length
$\ge c \log^{1/10} N$ among the first $N$ bits; Crandall (\emph{INTEGERS} 12 (2012) \#A23, \S7)
records the longest observed $0$-run as $47$ in the first $2^{43}$ bits and states that he does not
know how to show that \texttt{11} appears infinitely often. Campbell (arXiv:2605.24160, 2026)
settles that occurrence question affirmatively. \ev{Cited}
\end{rem}

That last pair of citations is the sharpest single orientation fact available. Every rigorous
result in the literature about the digits of $E$ points the wrong way: they produce long runs, or
guarantee that patterns occur. What (H) needs is that a pattern \emph{never} occurs for long. No
result of that logical type exists.

\section{The wall}
\label{sec:257-wall}

\subsection{The thesis}

This programme has produced many reformulations of \#257 and no proof of it. That is the honest
summary, and expert readers have said so. The claim of this section is that the reformulations are
not a hundred separate failures. They are repeated measurements of \emph{one} obstruction, taken in
different coordinates, and once they are assembled the obstruction has a describable shape. A
systematic elimination that says exactly which classes of argument cannot work, and why, is a
result about the problem; a reformulation is not. The elimination is what follows.

The generating fact is a single structural one, and it is not a convenience of the formalisation.

\begin{obs}[The problem admits no ensemble]
\label{obs:no-ensemble}
The Mersenne weights are strictly superincreasing. Consequently the digit coding is injective
(Theorem~\ref{thm:geometry}), the greedy orbit is the unique candidate support for any target, and
distinct subset sums of a row of seam weights are separated by at least $2^{s+1}$. Every one of the
nine coordinates the corpus has built --- truncation rung $J$, seam row $s$, integer margin, sharp
tail margin, half-carry $K_n$, reverse-carry word, Boolean--M\"obius exact row, fatal-cell interval
packing, Dedekind cut-locator --- is therefore a coordinate on the binary digits of \emph{one}
specific real orbit. There is no family to range over.
\coord{meta} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}\ev{Math}\\
\textbf{Sites:} \lean{weightedBoolSum\_separated}{HalfCylinderIntegerGreedy.lean:447},
\lean{three\_mul\_tailWeight\_add\_exactLateGap\_eq\_three\_mul\_headWeight}{HalfCylinderLargestSkipGap.lean:230},
\lean{positiveMersenneDigitValue\_injective}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:1582}.
\end{obs}

All six barriers below are consequences of Observation~\ref{obs:no-ensemble} in different
directions: there is nothing to choose (\ref{bar:canon}), nothing weaker to prove
(\ref{bar:collapse}), nothing to average over (\ref{bar:measure}), no information at the available
scales (\ref{bar:scale}), no certificate in the provable direction (\ref{bar:asym}), and, on the
universal side, no way to make a general support satisfy the certificate engine's first hypothesis
(\ref{bar:box}).

\subsection{Barrier 1: canonicalisation --- the witness space is a singleton}
\label{bar:canon}

\begin{prop}[Canonicalisation; \textsc{proved}]
\label{prop:canon}
For the half-target in strictly superincreasing Mersenne weights, every finite object satisfying an
approximate-hit condition at depth $d$ \emph{is} the canonical greedy prefix at depth $d$.
Precisely: (i) every straddling word at depth $d$ agrees bit-for-bit with the greedy support of
$1/2$ on $(0,d]$; (ii) every below-half core $D$ whose crossing deficit is smaller than the next
weight is forced to equal $\texttt{halfGreedyPrefixSupport}(c-1)$, so the universally quantified
$\forall D$ in the lane's headline socket collapses onto one orbit; (iii) a remainder below the gap
is equivalent to being the integer greedy bit-word.
\coord{straddle-prefix / integer-greedy} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}\\
\textbf{Sites:} \lean{IsStraddlePrefix.half\_agrees\_greedy}{HalfCutLocator.lean:442};
\lean{eq\_halfGreedyPrefixSupport\_of\_critical\_crossing}{BooleanMobiusCriticalCapacityCofinal.lean:50};
\lean{remainder\_lt\_gap\_iff\_eq\_integerGreedyBits}{BooleanMobiusGreedyReduction.lean:918}.
\end{prop}

\textbf{Rules out.} Every argument whose leverage comes from \emph{choosing} a favourable witness:
a support $D$, a Boolean word, a straddle prefix, an adversarial late word, or a lattice/covering
configuration. At each depth the admissible set has one element, so there is no choice to exploit
and no adversary to defeat.

\textbf{Explains.} Three recorded attack families die for exactly this reason, and their deaths
look superficially unrelated. (1) \emph{Generic lattice covering} proves the wrong sign: the free
prefix lattice breaches the $2^{t/2}$ safety radius in $74\%$ of $t \in [8,26]$ --- witness
$t = 15$, where fans of $\{2,3,5,6,10\}$ sum to $2^{15}+1$, at circular distance $1$; expected
near-hits grow like $2^{t/6}$. Any generic bound on prefix cancellation therefore proves the
opposite of what is wanted. \ev{Cert}

(2) \emph{Digit-cascade impossibility} is false statically:
explicit words exist at $t = 20$ (top $18$ of $20$ bits all-$1$) and $t = 22$ (top $20$ of $22$
all-$0$), and an unconditional construction supplies about $t/12$ independent single-bit knobs,
verified with zero exceptions to $t = 601$. \ev{Cert}

(3) \emph{Integer-window static adversary
search} finds nothing: zero hits, exhaustively for $t \le 22$ and exactly at $t = 40, 42, 45$ over
all cut positions. \ev{Cert}

The reconciliation is the content: residues mod $2^t$ are
adversary-tunable, but the integer \emph{magnitude} is not, because the row weights are
gap-dominated. So the static tier and the dynamic tier coincide identically, and all three families
were, without knowing it, measuring the same single orbit.

\begin{rem}[A recorded hazard]
The natural sufficient condition via fractional split-mass is \emph{not} necessary on real crossing
cores: the fixture $D = \{2,3\}$, $c = 5$ is an explicit counterexample
(\lean{splitFractionMass\_one\_bound\_not\_necessary\_fixture}{BooleanMobiusSkippedCoreCriticalCapacity.lean:183}).
Any attack on the capacity socket that routes through fractional mass is already refuted.
\ev{Lean}
\end{rem}

\subsection{Barrier 2: route collapse --- every ``weaker producer'' is the goal}
\label{bar:collapse}

This is the barrier that most consistently wastes effort, because the sockets it kills look
strictly weaker on the page: they are $\Pi^0_2$ statements ($\forall N \exists n \dots$) with
explicitly relaxed compatibility requirements, while the goal is $\Pi^0_1$. The extra quantifier
buys nothing. The mechanism is a single exact identity plus a single unconditional envelope.

\begin{lem}[Collapse mechanism; \textsc{proved}]
\label{lem:collapse-mech}
Let $A \subseteq \N$ with $1 \notin A$, put $\delta := 1/2 - x_A$, and let
$\operatorname{ihc}(A,N)$ denote the integer half-carry at level $N$. Then
\[
  \operatorname{ihc}(A,N) \;=\; 2^{N+1}\,\delta \;+\; \mathrm{T}(N+1),
  \qquad 0 \;\le\; \mathrm{T}(N+1) \;\le\; 2\sqrt{N+1} + 4 ,
\]
where $\mathrm{T}$ is the binary coefficient tail of $\operatorname{sc}_A$.
\coord{half-carry} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}\\
\textbf{Sites:} \lean{integerHalfCarry\_eq\_scaled\_residual\_add\_tail}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:871};
\lean{binaryCoeffTail\_nonneg}{GenericTailOrbitRigidity.lean:78};
\lean{binaryCoeffTail\_supportCoeff\_le\_two\_sqrt\_add\_four}{BooleanMobiusCarry.lean:290}.
\end{lem}

\begin{prop}[Route collapse; \textsc{proved}]
\label{prop:collapse}
Any hypothesis asserting, for cofinally many $N$, that $\operatorname{ihc}(A,N)$ lies inside a
strip of width $O(\sqrt{N})$ forces $\delta = 0$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse-mech}, $2^{N+1}\delta = \operatorname{ihc}(A,N) - \mathrm{T}(N+1)$.
For the greedy support of $1/2$ one has $x_G \le 1/2$, hence $\delta \ge 0$
(\lean{erdosSupportSeries\_greedyMersenneSupport\_le}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:888}). If
$\operatorname{ihc}(A,N) \le c\sqrt{N} + c'$ for cofinally many $N$, then
$2^{N+1}\delta \le c\sqrt{N} + c'$ along that sequence; since $2^{N+1}$ outgrows any $\sqrt{N}$
envelope and $\delta \ge 0$ is a fixed real, $\delta = 0$. Conversely $\delta = 0$ makes
$\operatorname{ihc}(A,N) = \mathrm{T}(N+1)$ exactly, and the unconditional envelope of
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse-mech} then \emph{supplies} the strip for free. And $\delta = 0$ is exactly
$1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$, by
\lean{mem\_mersenneAchievementSet\_iff\_greedy\_survival}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:1458}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[Perfect-square strip witness; \textsc{proved}, this pass]
\label{lem:sqrt-witness}
Suppose $x_A = 1/2$ and $1 \notin A$. Then for every $k \ge 1$,
\[
  \operatorname{ihc}(A, k^2 - 1) \;=\; \mathrm{T}(k^2) \;\le\; 2k + 4 \;=\; \texttt{halfStripBound}(k^2).
\]
\coord{half-carry} \scale{cofinal} \ev{Math}
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse-mech} with $\delta = 0$, $\operatorname{ihc}(A,k^2-1) = \mathrm{T}(k^2)
\le 2\sqrt{k^2} + 4$. At a perfect square the real and truncated square roots agree exactly,
$\sqrt{k^2} = k = \lfloor \sqrt{k^2} \rfloor$, so the real envelope coincides with the discrete
strip $\texttt{halfStripBound}(n) = 2\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor + 4$
(\lean{halfStripBound}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:32}) with no slack required.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:sqrt-witness} matters because it removes the last technical obstruction previously
recorded against two of the sockets below --- the mismatch between $\lfloor\sqrt{\cdot}\rfloor$ and
$\sqrt{\cdot}$, for which the corpus carries only the wrong-direction inequality. Restricting the
cofinal witness depths to perfect squares dissolves it, and no widening of the strip constant from
$4$ to $6$ is needed. For the terminal-only socket, take $a$ to be the indicator of an achieving
support truncated to $\{0,\dots,M\}$ at $M = k^2$, using
\lean{integerHalfCarry\_inter\_Iic\_eq\_of\_succ\_le}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:49} and
$1 \notin A$ (which holds since $x_1 = 1 > 1/2$).

\begin{prop}[The collapsed sockets; \textsc{proved}, composite]
\label{prop:collapsed-list}
Each of the following is logically \emph{equivalent} to $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$, not strictly weaker:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item the \texttt{hbound} hypothesis of
  \lean{greedy\_half\_infinite\_of\_mobiusCenteredHalfCarry\_sqrtBound}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:834}
  and of \lean{greedy\_half\_infinite\_of\_mobiusCenteredHalfCarry\_upperBound}{HalfCarryReachability.lean:953};
\item \lean{CofinalPositiveHalfGreedySkips}{BooleanMobiusSkipRowCofinal.lean:22};
\item \lean{CofinalExactLocalMersenneHalfRows}{BooleanMobiusCofinalExactRows.lean:38}, including its
  advertised weakening that \emph{no} compatibility is required between witness supports at
  different endpoints;
\item \lean{GreedyHalfCarryCofinalStripReturn}{CofinalStripReturn.lean:76};
\item \lean{HalfCarryCofinalTerminalOnlyStrip}{TerminalOnlyCofinal.lean:34};
\item the negation of \lean{ExistsFatalHalfGap}{HalfCutLocator.lean:526}, and all seven forms of the
  associated classification chain.
\end{enumerate}
Forward directions are landed Lean (e.g.\ \lean{half\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet\_iff\_greedySkippedSupport\_infinite}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:2583},
\lean{half\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet\_of\_cofinalExactLocalRows}{BooleanMobiusCofinalExactRows.lean:71},
\lean{existsFatalHalfGap\_iff\_half\_not\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet}{HalfCutLocator.lean:643}).
The reverse directions of (d) and (e) are derived here from Lemma~\ref{lem:sqrt-witness} and are
\emph{not} themselves landed Lean theorems.
\coord{half-carry / exact-row} \scale{cofinal} \ev{Lean}\ev{Math}
\end{prop}

\textbf{Rules out.} Every strategy of the form ``the full problem is hard, so prove this weaker
sufficient condition instead''. In particular the specific hope that mutually \emph{incompatible}
witnesses at different endpoints buy flexibility is void, and reading the $\Pi^0_2$ shape of these
sockets as evidence of extra room is an error.

\textbf{Scope limit, preserved exactly.} Item (a) does \emph{not} extend to every $A$ with
$1 \notin A$. Dropping the greedy-specific fact $x_G \le 1/2$ yields only the one-sided statement
$\texttt{hbound} \iff (1/2 - x_A) \le 0$. This limit must be stated verbatim wherever the
equivalence is used.

\subsection{Barrier 3: scale mismatch --- the available information is at the wrong resolution}
\label{bar:scale}

The input the half-value branch actually needs is a per-index, growing-precision,
\emph{short-window} near-integer anti-concentration bound: at reset row $r$, a lower bound on the
distance from $\Z$ of $\sum_{i \le L} \delta(M+i)\,2^{-i}$ with $\delta(M) = \tau(M) - 1$, where the
precision demanded grows like $2^{-r/2}$ while the window length is only $L \approx r/2$. Every
information source tried lives at one of two extremes, and provably cannot reach that intermediate
scale.

\textbf{Class (a): fixed precision or bounded state.} Three landed theorems, all
problem-agnostic --- they mention no Mersenne structure and bind \#249 identically.

\begin{prop}[Local information is void; \textsc{proved}]
\label{prop:local-void}
(i) After $L$ common steps the endpoint residue mod $2^L$ of an affine binary orbit is
\emph{independent} of the initial carry: $u(L) - v(L) = 2^L(u_0 - v_0)$
(\lean{affineBinaryOrbit\_mod\_twoPow\_eq}{GenericTailOrbitRigidity.lean:307}). (ii) No bounded or
autonomous state summarising the history before position $m$ determines the tail after $m$: for a
balanced-pulse family no decoder recovers the radius, and any finite state type needs cardinality
at least $\lfloor m/2 \rfloor + 2$, unbounded in $m$
(\lean{balancedPulse\_no\_autonomous\_decoder}{GenericTailOrbitRigidity.lean:247}). (iii) For any
starting carry and any finite word of odd valuation-unit symbols at fixed precision $u > 0$, a
compatible centred carry completion \emph{exists}
(\lean{fixedPrecisionTropicalNoGo}{TropicalCurvatureCarry.lean:137}), so fixed-precision local data
never excludes anything. \coord{carry} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}
\end{prop}

The parity route is the same fact by hand: ``$K(M)$ even $\iff$ $M$ a perfect square'' is true, but
$J(M+1) = 2J(M) - \delta(M+1)$ makes $J(M+1) \equiv \delta(M+1) \pmod 2$ an identity for
\emph{any} digit stream whatsoever. It is a tautology of the recursion shape; it forbids no residue
and no run. \ev{Math}

\textbf{Class (b): global averages and Diophantine data.}

\begin{prop}[Exponent-budget mismatch; \textsc{proved}, given a cited $\mu$]
\label{prop:exponent-gap}
The skip set at row $n$ has $|\mathrm{Skip}_n| \approx n^2/4$, so an irrationality-measure exponent
$\mu$ for the target constant yields a separation of order only $2^{-\mu n^2/4}$, while the
required precision is $2^{-3n/2}$. The shortfall is a factor $\approx 0.42\,n$ \emph{in the
exponent}, and it grows linearly in $n$. Since $\mu \ge 2$ for every irrational, no improvement in
$\mu$ can close it: Zudilin's $\mu(E) \le 2.42343562\ldots$ is irrelevant to the gap, and so is any
future bound. \coord{diophantine} \scale{cofinal} \ev{Math}\ev{Cited}
\end{prop}

\textbf{Rules out.} Any argument deriving the run-length or magnitude bound from (a) arithmetic
information at fixed finite precision or a bounded automaton state, or (b) a global average,
density, or Diophantine-approximation quantity attached to $C = E - 3/2$. Concretely: no proposal
to bound the R-run via an irrationality measure of a Mersenne or Erd\H{o}s--Borwein-type constant
can work, however good the measure becomes; and no proposal defining a bounded carry state
summarising pre-index history can determine the post-index tail.

\textbf{Explains.} Prime-doubling fails for a recorded reason rather than for want of effort: $N$
prime $\ge n$ gives $b(N) = 1$ so $J$ doubles at that step, but not cumulatively, because
composites subtract. The explicit witness is the quiet run at $n = 607$, spanning
$M \in (607,617]$ with $J \in [1,5]$ for eleven steps \emph{while three of those indices --- $607$,
$613$, $617$ --- are themselves prime}; and $26$ of the $40$ longest runs with $n \le 1500$ contain
a prime. No run-length bound follows from prime counting. \ev{Cert}

\begin{rem}[Status of the exhaustion claim]
The seven named families are each closed with mechanism. The claim that classes (a) and (b)
\emph{exhaust} the available tools is an \textsc{observed pattern}, not a theorem, and is labelled
as such wherever it is used below.
\end{rem}

\subsection{Barrier 4: decision asymmetry --- the certificates point the wrong way}
\label{bar:asym}

\begin{thm}[One-sidedness; \textsc{proved}]
\label{thm:one-sided}
$1/2 \notin \mathcal{A}$ is $\Sigma^0_1$: a single finite object --- a fatal gap --- certifies it.
$1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$ is $\Pi^0_1$, and no finite object certifies it. Consequently no finite
computation, at any depth, can establish $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$.
\coord{meta} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
The dichotomy $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$ or $\exists$ a fatal gap is unconditional
(\lean{half\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet\_or\_exists\_fatal\_gap}{HalfCutLocator.lean:623}), and the
two alternatives are exactly complementary
(\lean{existsFatalHalfGap\_iff\_half\_not\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet}{HalfCutLocator.lean:643}),
with \lean{ExistsFatalHalfGap}{HalfCutLocator.lean:526} a finite witness type. In the other
direction, certified death implies non-membership
(\lean{certifiedGreedyMersenneDeath\_not\_mem}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:1762}, over
\lean{CertifiedGreedyMersenneDeath}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:1756}; $3/4$ is certified dead at
level $1$, lookahead $0$, by
\lean{three\_fourths\_certifiedGreedyMersenneDeath}{GreedyAchievementSet.lean:1778}), whereas the
absence of such a certificate at any depth $D$ is compatible with a certificate at depth $D+1$ and
therefore proves nothing. Membership asserts survival at \emph{every} rank
(Theorem~\ref{thm:greedy-survival}), an infinite conjunction with no finite sub-conjunction
implying it.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Rules out.} Any programme whose plan is ``extend the certified depth''. Pushing the
truncation rung to $J = 23$, the seam to row $10^6$, or the macro receipt to depth $10^8$ cannot in
principle decide the branch --- each raises a lower bound on a death rank which, if the supported
branch is correct, does not exist.

\textbf{Explains.} Every certificate the programme owns is of this one type. The certified depths
grew by roughly two orders of magnitude across the recorded history with no change whatsoever in
decision status; that is not a coincidence to be pushed through, it is the theorem above being
observed empirically. Accumulated survival is not convergence toward proof.

\subsection{Barrier 5: measure and category point in opposite directions}
\label{bar:measure}

\begin{prop}[The two soft heuristics contradict each other; \textsc{proved}]
\label{prop:soft}
$\mathcal{A}$ has Lebesgue measure exactly $1$ inside an ambient span of $E \approx 1.6067$, a
relative density of about $62\%$, and is simultaneously nowhere dense, perfect and totally
disconnected (Theorem~\ref{thm:geometry}). Hence the measure heuristic --- ``a random point lies in
$\mathcal{A}$ with probability $\approx 0.62$'' --- predicts \#257 \emph{false}, while the category
heuristic --- ``$\mathcal{A}$ is meagre, so a generic point misses it'' --- predicts \#257
\emph{true}. \coord{achievement-set} \scale{uniform} \ev{Lean}
\end{prop}

\textbf{Rules out.} Every soft argument: measure-theoretic, Baire-category, metric number theory
(almost-all / almost-none), and probabilistic digit models. The two standard notions of ``typical''
disagree on this object, so neither can be the mechanism. Worse, by injectivity of the digit coding
(Observation~\ref{obs:no-ensemble}) there is no ensemble of candidate representations to average
over: each rational has exactly one candidate support, so counting, second-moment and pigeonhole
arguments have nothing to range over. It also rules out deciding (U) by showing $\mathcal{A}$ is
``small'': it is not small --- it is $62\%$ of its ambient interval by measure.

\textbf{Explains.} The programme's own probabilistic estimate sits squarely on the losing side of
this split: under a Bernoulli$(1/2)$ digit model the expected number of counterexamples with
$n \ge 32$ is about $2 \times 10^{-4}$, i.e.\ the model predicts $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$. That
estimate is worth exactly as much as the model, and the model is a measure-side object that cannot
see a specific point of a nowhere-dense set having Lebesgue measure exactly $1$. \ev{Cert}

\subsection{Barrier 6: the two-axis certificate box (universal direction)}
\label{bar:box}

\begin{obs}[The box; \textsc{observed pattern}]
\label{obs:box}
The support-dependent CRT certificate constructions have two separate jobs:
prescribe enough divisor incidences to control a block, and bound the
remaining tail so that $q(C+N+L+2)<b^L$. Pairwise-coprime petals and a
summable reciprocal budget are sufficient inputs in the corresponding
construction. These are requirements of that producer, not necessary
conditions for irrationality. The small-shift method in
\S\ref{sec:257-weighted-returns} uses neither a pairwise-coprime support nor
ordinary reciprocal summability.
\coord{certificate-engine} \ev{Math}
\end{obs}

\textbf{What a general-support application still needs.} Shared factors
impose compatibility conditions on CRT prescriptions; a proof must check
those conditions and supply its tail estimate. The existing hypotheses
cannot simply be omitted from the proof that uses them. This observation
does not prove that all possible certificates or CRT constructions fail.

\textbf{Explains only a method boundary.} The primes are infinite and pairwise coprime
--- axis (i) is satisfied in the cleanest possible way, and
\lean{compositeDilationDefect\_eq\_zero\_of\_prime\_support}{CompositeDilationDefect.lean:103} makes
the dilation algebra exact there --- yet this certificate theorem does not
apply because $\sum1/p$ diverges by Mertens and axis~(ii) fails.  This explains
the absence of a \emph{Lean theorem from this engine}; it does not make the
base-$2$ value open, since Tao--Ter\"av\"ainen settle it by a different
analytic method.  The sunflower generalisation inherits both axes and
therefore does not recover that external theorem.

\subsection{What the six barriers converge on}

Several remaining approaches ask for arithmetic control of geometrically
weighted divisor incidences. For the half-value probe one proposed input
concerns $\sum_{i\le L}(\tau(M+i)-1)2^{-i}$; for a general support a block
certificate instead concerns its own incidence $\operatorname{sc}_A$ and
tail budget. Their similar appearance suggests useful comparisons, but does
not make the two producers equivalent. The weighted and fractional-cover
theorems show that small positive shifts can also be obtained without
constructing either prescribed block.

\section{What the wall does not block}
\label{sec:257-survivors}

Six barriers eliminate a great deal. What they do not eliminate is a short, specific list, and the
point of the elimination is that this list is short. Each route below is given with the exact
statement it needs, the barrier it evades, and \emph{why} it evades it. Where a route evades every
proved barrier but sits inside the class indicted by an observed pattern, that is said plainly.

\subsection{Route 1: the reset \texorpdfstring{\(\sqrt{\ }\)}{square-root}-escape / R-run anti-concentration target}
\label{ssec:route-1}

\textbf{What it needs.} Any one of three forms, in decreasing strength.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Run-length form.} For every reset row $r \ge 31$ the maximal pure-R run starting at $r$
  satisfies $L_r < (r-3)/2$; equivalently $|\mathrm{rem}(r+1) - 2^{r+1}| > 2^{(r+5)/2}$. Any
  sublinear bound $L_r = o(r)$ suffices.
\item \emph{Lean-facing form, already fanned in.} \texttt{SeamUpperResetDyadicBandEscape}: for every
  $d \ge 13$ with \texttt{successorCarries} and every $j \le d$, the reset charge
  $4\cdot\text{overshoot} + \text{abovePulse}$ avoids the linear-width band
  $(2^{d-j+1} - 2(d+j),\, 2^{d-j+1}]$.
\item \emph{Sign law alone.} At every reset, M-resets have $\mathrm{dev} > 0$ and U-resets have
  $\mathrm{dev} < 0$. Strictly weaker than either inequality above, and flagged in its own source as
  worth attempting first.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Sites.} \lean{SeamUpperResetDyadicBandEscape}{HalfCylinderMiddleCarryLowerBound.lean:4566}
with consumer
\lean{half\_mem\_mersenneAchievementSet\_of\_upperResetDyadicBandEscape}{HalfCylinderMiddleCarryLowerBound.lean:4790};
quantifier collapse \lean{dyadicBandEscape\_iff\_exists\_critical}{HalfUpperResetCriticalBand.lean:108},
which reduces the $\forall j \in [0,d]$ band check to one nearest-boundary index per row; finite
verification \lean{seamUpperResetDyadicBandEscape\_through\_thirty}{HalfCylinderUpperResetBandCertificates.lean:78};
alternative fan-in \lean{LargestSkipLateStepSocket}{HalfCylinderLargestSkipInduction.lean:34} with
consumer at \texttt{:167} and base case
\lean{largestSkipLateAt\_fourteen}{HalfCylinderLargestSkipInduction.lean:73}.

\textbf{Why it evades Barrier~\ref{bar:collapse}.} This is the one reduction in the corpus that is
genuinely \emph{sufficient} rather than equivalent. It is a quantitative bound at every reset row,
while $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$ is qualitative; it can be false while the goal is true. There is no
implication $1/2 \in \mathcal{A} \Rightarrow \sqrt{\ }$-escape anywhere in the corpus, and by the
shape of the two statements there cannot be. Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}'s mechanism needs a
strip hypothesis of width $O(\sqrt N)$ around the half-carry; this route asserts a lower bound on a
deviation, which the collapse identity does not manufacture.

\textbf{Why it evades Barrier~\ref{bar:asym}.} It is $\Pi^0_1$ with certificates aligned with the
branch being proved. The margins \emph{grow}: $+1.1119$ bits at row $14$, about $1246$ bits by row
$2500$, mean $627$, never re-approaching the threshold. So for once the finite computation is
evidence for the target rather than for its unprovable complement. \ev{Cert}

\textbf{Why it evades Barrier~\ref{bar:canon}.} It is a statement about the one canonical orbit ---
exactly the object canonicalisation says everything reduces to. Here the singleton witness space is
help, not obstruction: there is no adversary to beat, only one sequence to bound.

\textbf{The trap it avoids.} A \emph{uniform constant} run-length bound is empirically false: runs
grow like $\log_2(\text{row})$. The route survives because any $L_r = o(r)$ suffices, and the
observed slack is enormous --- maximum R-run $19$ at row $158{,}096$ against a requirement of
$(r-3)/2 = 79{,}046$, a margin of roughly $4000\times$. \ev{Cert}

\textbf{What it does not evade.} Barrier~\ref{bar:scale}. Its residual difficulty \emph{is} the
missing object of \S2.7. This route and that barrier are the same frontier viewed from the two
sides, which is the honest reason it is listed first: it is where the real mathematics has to be
done, not a way around it. \ev{Open}

\subsection{Route 2: strict endpoint progress for exact rows}
\label{ssec:route-2}

\textbf{What it needs.} Rule out the recycle branch returning to a bounded endpoint forever. Any
one of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item \codeid{SkippedCoreCriticalQuotientSupply}: for every below-half core $D$ bounded by $[2,c)$

\item a direct proof that the crossing rank $c$ produced by the recycle branch cannot repeat the

\item a growth law for $c$ as a function of the incoming endpoint $n$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:canon}(ii), form (a) need only be checked along the \emph{one} canonical

biconditional it is exactly the $c-2$ bit capacity test
$\texttt{localBinarySuffix}\,D\,1\,(2c-2) < 2^{c-2}$. It must \emph{not} be attacked via the
fractional-mass route, which the fixture $D = \{2,3\}$, $c=5$ refutes as non-necessary.

\textbf{Sites.} \lean{exactLocalMersenneHalfRow\_double\_or\_recycle}{BooleanMobiusExactRowDichotomy.lean:26};

\lean{exists\_seeded\_bounded\_double\_or\_recycle\_model}{BooleanMobiusExactRowDichotomy.lean:91}

\lean{exists\_skippedCoreExactRow\_of\_value\_above}{BooleanMobiusExactRowCrossing.lean:155};

\lean{localBinarySuffix\_two\_mul\_sub\_two\_lt\_criticalCapacity\_iff}{BooleanMobiusSkippedCoreCriticalCapacity.lean:22};

\textbf{Why it evades Barrier~\ref{bar:canon}.} This is the single place where canonicalisation
provably does \emph{not} bite, and the reason is exact. Proposition~\ref{prop:canon}(ii) requires
both a real-valued below-half condition \emph{and} the deficit clause. The exact-row predicate
\texttt{ExactLocalMersenneHalfRow} imposes neither: it asks only that a floor quotient hit
$2^{n-1}-1$ in $\N$. Floor division is not injective. So the per-endpoint witness space is an
exponential search over $D \subseteq [2,n]$, is decidable for each $n$, and is not forced to be the
greedy prefix. It is the only genuine degree of freedom left anywhere in the half-value branch.

\textbf{Its relation to Barriers~\ref{bar:collapse} and \ref{bar:asym}.} The \emph{cofinal}
statement is still equivalent to the goal, by Proposition~\ref{prop:collapsed-list}(c). But
equivalence of truth value is not equivalence of proof difficulty when the existential has an
exponentially large witness space rather than a singleton. And decidability of
\texttt{ExactLocalMersenneHalfRow}$(n)$ makes the exact-row set computable, so the data here is a
two-sided observable rather than a one-sided survival lower bound --- a real softening of
Barrier~\ref{bar:asym}, though not an escape from it.

\subsection{Route 3 audit: the prime-support theorem is known outside this formal corpus}
\label{ssec:route-3}

The base-$2$ prime-support value is not an open target:
\[
  \sum_{p\ {\rm prime}}\frac1{2^p-1}
  =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\omega(n)}{2^n}
\]
by expanding each reciprocal Mersenne term geometrically and collecting
divisors.  Tao and Ter\"av\"ainen prove the right-hand series irrational in
Theorem~1.3 of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01739}{\emph{Quantitative correlations and
some problems on prime factors of consecutive integers}}.  Their proof is
analytic and is cited here; it is not formalised in the pinned Lean corpus.

The local pairwise-coprime certificate theorem still does not cover the
primes because it assumes summability of $\sum_p1/p$.  That is a boundary of
this engine, not a boundary of the mathematics.  The previously proposed
windowed-tail repair is therefore withdrawn as a research contribution.  It
may remain useful as an audit question about whether the existing Lean engine
can reproduce the known theorem, but reproducing a cited result is not new
progress on \#257.  The authors mention broader base and prime-power
extensions with modifications omitted in the cited version; this paper
records only the fully proved base-$2$ theorem.

\subsection{Route 4: land the route-collapse equivalences}
\label{ssec:route-4}

\textbf{What it needs.} Five biconditionals, each with $1/2 \in \mathcal{A}$ on the right-hand

\texttt{CofinalPositiveHalfGreedySkips}; \texttt{CofinalExactLocalMersenneHalfRows};

\subsection{Route 5: the sparse/dense trichotomy closure}
\label{ssec:route-5}

\textbf{What it needs.} An unconditional
\[
  A \text{ infinite} \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \big(\text{cofinally many super-logarithmic zero windows in } \operatorname{sc}_A\big)
  \;\vee\;
  \big(\forall q > 0,\ \text{a weighted block certificate exists for } \operatorname{sc}_A\big).
\]
The first disjunct contradicts
\lean{supportCoeffZeroWindow\_length\_le\_eps\_logb}{SublogDivisorCoverage.lean:435} whenever the
series is rational; the second closes via the certificate engine. The sparse branch is done ---
that theorem, and independently
\lean{irrational\_erdosSum\_of\_lcm\_gap}{CertificateKernel.lean:5883}, close it. What is required
is the \emph{dense-branch producer}: for $A$ whose divisor incidence is bounded below infinitely
often, construct a block of $K$ consecutive indices with
$2^K \mid \sum_{r=1}^{K} \operatorname{sc}_A(N+r)\,2^{K-r}$, a bounded middle window $C$, and
$q(C+N+L+2) < 2^L$.

\textbf{Why it evades Barrier~\ref{bar:box}.} It attacks the box from outside rather than
instantiating inside it. It does not ask a general support to satisfy axis (i) or (ii); it asks for
a dichotomy on the support's own divisor density, and then uses a different tool on each side. It
evades Barrier~\ref{bar:measure} because it is a structural statement about the incidence function,
not a measure or category claim.

\textbf{The honest flag.} The dense-branch producer is the same missing object as \S2.7, restated
in the incidence coordinate. That is not a reason to discard the route --- the restatement is
genuinely a different coordinate, and a proof might be easier there --- but it is a reason not to
count it as an independent line of attack. \ev{Open}

\subsection{Ranking, stated plainly}

Route 4 is the only one that can be completed with the mathematics currently in hand; it produces
theorems, not progress on the wall. Route 3 is the only one that would produce a new irrationality
theorem for a named constant, and its missing input is a windowed Mertens estimate, which is
ordinary analytic number theory rather than a new phenomenon. Route 2 is the only place in the
half-value branch with a non-singleton witness space, which makes it the best target for search.
Route 1 is where the actual obstruction lives, and Route 5 is Route 1 in another coordinate.
Neither problem is close to being solved, and no route above is a proof of anything.

\section{Reading the detailed record}
\label{sec:257-howto}

The opening note has already defined the evidence bands, scale tags,
coordinates, and catalogue terminology.  The longer record has two different
reading modes, and they should not be confused.

\paragraph{Read linearly.} Sections~\ref{sec:257-wall} and
\ref{sec:257-survivors} form one argument: the first identifies the recurring
obstructions, and the second isolates the routes those obstructions do not
exclude.  Read them in order before selecting a new attack.

\paragraph{Use as reference.} The mathematical-ingredient catalogue may be
entered by coordinate: the certificate method and universal-direction record;
the greedy and achievement-set geometry; the seam model, master identity, and
dynamics; the half-carry and Boolean--M\"obius coordinates; and the support
rigidity results.  The later implication tables record what chains with what,
while the near-miss index gives the exact mismatch at each open obligation.

\paragraph{Scale and coordinate discipline.} The direct certified instances
remain fixed or bounded --- rungs $J \le 22$, seam rows $\le 200{,}000$, ranks
$\le 3000$, and macro depth $\le 13{,}548{,}057$ --- whereas the open
obligations are cofinal.  Theorem~\ref{thm:one-sided} shows that extending a
bounded range does not establish a cofinal statement.  Coordinate tags are
equally literal: an obstruction in \coord{half-carry}, \coord{seam-row}, or
\coord{diophantine} does not transfer to another representation without a
proved map.  Coordinate-free barriers are marked explicitly.
