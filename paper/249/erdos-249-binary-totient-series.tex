\begin{abstract}
The totient sections through level $e\ge1$ in an integer base $k\ge2$
span a rational vector space of dimension $k^e+1$. We give a basis and
determine all integral relations among the indexed sections. The proof
uses the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem to isolate
one affine totient value at a time. The same construction gives
irrationality results for bounded functions of totient residues.
A final section states a sufficient exponential-sum estimate for the
unresolved irrationality of $\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(n)2^{-n}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:results}
\papersectiontarget{short249-basis}
We study the linear relations among the sequences
$n\mapsto\varphi(k^jn+r)$, where $k\ge2$, $j\ge0$ and $0\le r<k^j$.
Here $\varphi$ is Euler's totient, $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ and
$\varphi(0)=0$. At base two the identities
\[
 \varphi(4n)=2\varphi(2n),\qquad
 \varphi(4n+2)=\varphi(2n+1)
\]
reduce the seven indexed sections through level two to five.
We shall prove that these five are independent, and that in every base
the corresponding scalar reductions account for all relations.

The sections form the $k$-kernel of the totient sequence. A sequence is
$k$-regular when the $\Z$-module generated by its $k$-kernel is finitely
generated~\cite{allouche-shallit}. Coons proved that the totient sequence is
not $k$-regular for any $k\ge2$~\cite[Thm.~3.2]{coons}.
Our result specifies the rank at each finite level and gives integral
coordinates for both the sections and their relations.

\begin{theorem}[A basis through each finite level]
\label{thm:kkernelrank}
Let $k\ge2$ and $e\ge1$ be integers, write $F^{(k)}_{j,r}(n)=\ph(k^jn+r)$, and put
\[
 V_{k,e}=\operatorname{span}_{\Q}
 \{\,F^{(k)}_{j,r}:0\le j\le e,\ 0\le r<k^j\,\}.
\]
Then $\dim_{\Q}V_{k,e}=k^e+1$, and
\[
 \mathcal B_{k,e}
 =\{F^{(k)}_{0,0},F^{(k)}_{1,0}\}
 \cup\{\,F^{(k)}_{j,r}:1\le j\le e,\ 1\le r<k^j,\ k\nmid r\,\}
\]
is a basis.  Every omitted section reduces to a basis element by an explicit
scalar: $F^{(k)}_{j,0}=k^{j-1}F^{(k)}_{1,0}$ for $j\ge1$, and if $r=k^tu$ with
$t=\max\{s:k^s\mid r\}\ge1$ and $k\nmid u$, then
\[
 F^{(k)}_{j,r}=C_k(t,u)\,F^{(k)}_{j-t,u},
 \qquad
 C_k(t,u)=k^t\prod_{\substack{p\mid k\\ p\nmid u}}\Bigl(1-\tfrac1p\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

For a composite base, we retain some residues having a common factor
with the base. At base six, for example, both $r=1$ and $r=2$ occur
in the basis, although the corresponding reductions have different
multipliers:
\[
 \varphi(36n+6)=2\varphi(6n+1),\qquad
 \varphi(36n+12)=4\varphi(6n+2).
\]
The factor changes because $6n+2$ already contains the prime $2$.
We use the same formula when the base has repeated prime factors,
so neither primality nor squarefreeness is required.

The independence of totients along pairwise nonproportional affine
forms already follows from Martin's 2006 theorem on simultaneous
totient inequalities~\cite[Thm.~1]{martin-phi-inequalities}.
We give a direct proof by constructing an evaluation matrix that is
nonsingular modulo a prime, using the CRT--Dirichlet separation argument
in Yazdani's proof of Theorem~2, credited there to
Shallit~\cite[pp.~652--653]{yazdani2001}.
Once independence is established, the local formula for $\varphi$
identifies every section omitted from the basis. Corollary~\ref{cor:integral-normal-form} records the
resulting relation module, and Section~\ref{sec:base-six-test} works it
out at base six.

Section~\ref{sec:family} applies prime isolation to bounded functions of
totient residues. The series
\[
 S=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\varphi(n)}{2^n}
\]
has unbounded coefficients. Its irrationality, asked in
Erd\H{o}s Problem~\#249~\cite[p.~61]{erdosgraham1980}, remains open.
The basis theorem concerns coefficient relations; it supplies no
irrationality conclusion for this value. Sections~\ref{sec:carry-rank}
and~\ref{sec:open} describe the additional tail estimate that would
suffice. Complete secondary arguments are collected in
\longrecord{long249-r3-complements}{\emph{The Binary Totient Series}}.

\section{Proof of the basis theorem}\label{sec:rank}
\papersectiontarget{short249-proof}
We first reduce the omitted sections. For $n>0$, multiplication of $kn$
by $k^{j-1}$ changes no prime divisor, so
$\varphi(k^jn)=k^{j-1}\varphi(kn)$; at $n=0$ both sides vanish.
If $r=k^tu>0$ with $k\nmid u$, each prime dividing $k$ either already
divides $k^{j-t}n+u$, or contributes one new factor $1-1/p$ upon
multiplication by $k^t$. The product formula for $\varphi$ gives
$C_k(t,u)$ and proves spanning.

For independence, let $L_i(n)=a_i n+b_i$ ($1\le i\le s$), where
$a_i>0$ and $a_i b_j-a_j b_i\ne0$ for $i\ne j$.
After shifting the variable we may assume that all $b_i>0$, and we
write $g_i=\gcd(a_i,b_i)$ and $A_i=L_i/g_i$.
We shall isolate column $i$ by making the primitive part $A_i(n)$
prime and forcing a suitable prime factor into every other form:
the latter factors will make all the off-diagonal totients vanish
modulo a fixed prime $\ell$.

Choose an odd prime $\ell$ dividing none of the $a_i$, the
$g_i\varphi(g_i)$ or the nonzero cross determinants.
For each fixed $i$, Dirichlet's theorem allows us to choose distinct
primes $q_{ij}\equiv1\pmod\ell$ ($j\ne i$) outside the same finite
exceptional set. The congruences
\[
 A_i(n)\equiv2\pmod\ell,
 \qquad L_j(n)\equiv0\pmod{q_{ij}}\quad(j\ne i)
\]
are individually soluble and have pairwise coprime moduli, so CRT
combines them into a progression $n=n_0+Ru$, where
$R=\ell\prod_{j\ne i}q_{ij}$.

To apply Dirichlet again, we must check that the induced progression
for $A_i(n)$ is reduced modulo $(a_i/g_i)R$.
Primitivity gives $\gcd(A_i(n),a_i/g_i)=1$, while the residue $2$
excludes $\ell$. If some $q_{ij}$ divided $A_i(n)$, it would divide
both $L_i(n)$ and $L_j(n)$ and hence their cross determinant
$a_i b_j-a_j b_i$, contrary to our choice of $q_{ij}$.
Since these account for every prime divisor of the modulus, Dirichlet
supplies arbitrarily large prime values $p=A_i(n)$, from which we
choose one with $p\nmid g_i$.

At the resulting point $n_i$,
\[
 \varphi(L_i(n_i))=\varphi(g_i)(p-1)
       \equiv\varphi(g_i)\not\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]
For $j\ne i$ we have $q_{ij}-1\mid\varphi(L_j(n_i))$, so every
other entry of row $i$ vanishes modulo $\ell$. We may choose the
points $n_i$ independently and beyond any prescribed threshold;
the resulting evaluation matrix is diagonal modulo $\ell$, with
nonzero diagonal entries, and therefore has a nonzero integer determinant.
This proves rational independence of the sequences $\varphi(L_i(n))$
after deletion of any finite prefix. In particular, the argument
requires only one prime value in each row.

The two zero-residue forms in $\mathcal B_{k,e}$ are proportional,
so we now treat them separately. Restrict a proposed relation to
$n=km+1$, where $\varphi(kn)=\varphi(k)\varphi(n)$.
The positive-residue sections become the forms
\[
 k^{j+1}m+k^j+r\quad(k\nmid r),
\]
together with $km+1$. These are pairwise nonproportional: equality of
two intercept-to-slope ratios would give
$r/k^j=r'/k^{j'}$, which for $j'>j$ forces $k\mid r'$.
No positive-residue form is proportional to $km+1$.
Applying affine independence now eliminates every positive-residue
coefficient and gives $A+B\varphi(k)=0$, where $A,B$ are the
coefficients of $\varphi(n),\varphi(kn)$.
We obtain the second equation $A+Bk=0$ by evaluating at $n=k$,
and $\varphi(k)<k$ then forces both coefficients to vanish. Finally,
\[
 \lvert\mathcal B_{k,e}\rvert
   =2+\sum_{j=1}^e(k^j-k^{j-1})=k^e+1,
\]
which completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:kkernelrank}.
For independence on a tail, use a sufficiently large power of $k$
in the last evaluation.

\begin{corollary}[Integral coordinates and all integral relations]\label{cor:integral-normal-form}
Let $k\ge2$ and $e\ge1$. The retained family is a $\Z$-basis of the
module generated by the sections through level $e$. Index the sections
by their level and residue, retaining distinct indices even when they
define equal sequences. For each omitted index $i$, write the scalar
reduction as $F_i=a_iF_{j(i)}$, where $j(i)$ is retained and $a_i$ is a
nonnegative integer. In the free abelian group with one generator $E_i$
for each of these indices, the vectors
\[
 R_i=E_i-a_iE_{j(i)}\qquad(i\text{ omitted})
\]
form a $\Z$-basis of the kernel of evaluation $E_i\mapsto F_i$.
In particular, every integral relation has a unique integral expression
in these elementary relations, and their rank is
$\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}k^j$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $\prod_{p\mid k,\,p\nmid u}p$ divides $k$, every scalar
$C_k(t,u)$ is an integer. The retained family therefore spans the
section module over $\Z$, and its rational independence makes it a
$\Z$-basis.

Let $x=\sum_i x_iE_i$ evaluate to zero. If we subtract
$\sum_{i\text{ omitted}}x_iR_i$, the remaining vector is supported
on retained indices and still evaluates to zero, so it vanishes by
their independence. Conversely, we recover $c_i$ from the coefficient
at omitted index $i$ in any combination $\sum_h c_hR_h$, proving
both independence of the $R_i$ and uniqueness of the expansion. Counting omitted indices gives
$(1+k+\cdots+k^e)-(k^e+1)=\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}k^j$.
\end{proof}
Note that the section module can be smaller than the group of
integer-valued sequences in its rational span. For $k=2$ and $e\ge2$,
for example, $\varphi(4n+3)/2$ is integer-valued but has coefficient
$1/2$ in the retained basis, so it does not belong to the module.
Thus saturation in all integer-valued sequences fails.

\begin{corollary}[Dyadic basis]\label{res:basis}
The family
\[
 \{\ph_{0,0},\ph_{1,0}\}
 \cup\{\ph_{j,r}:j\ge1,\ 0<r<2^j,\ r\text{ odd}\},
 \qquad \ph_{j,r}(n)=\ph(2^jn+r),
\]
is a basis for the rational span of the full dyadic kernel. Every rational
relation is generated by the scalar reductions. The complete level-zero
truncation has dimension one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each finite subset of this family is contained in some
$\mathcal B_{2,e}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:kkernelrank} gives independence,
and its reductions give spanning. At level zero the sole section
$\varphi(n)$ is nonzero, since $\varphi(1)=1$.
\end{proof}

The affine argument also permits periodic coefficients by restriction
to their common residue classes.
\begin{corollary}[Periodic coefficients]\label{cor:periodic-freezing}
Let $L_1,\ldots,L_s$ be pairwise nonproportional affine forms with integer
coefficients and positive slopes. If $w_1,\ldots,w_s$ are rational-valued
periodic sequences, then
\[
 \sum_i w_i(n)\ph(L_i(n))=0\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w_i(n)=0\quad\text{for every }i,n.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose a common period $Q$ and restrict to $n=r+Qt$, where the
coefficients become the constants $w_i(r)$ and the cross determinants
become $Q(a_i b_j-a_j b_i)\ne0$. We may shift $t$ beyond the given
threshold and apply affine independence to obtain $w_i(r)=0$ for
every $i$. Repeating this for each residue $r$ and using periodicity
proves the conclusion at every index.
\end{proof}
Proportional forms are excluded for a reason: the identity
$\varphi(4n+2)=\varphi(2n+1)$ would otherwise contradict the conclusion.
Martin's theorem gives the same corollary on each
progression~\cite[Thm.~1]{martin-phi-inequalities}.

\section{An equality test at base six}\label{sec:base-six-test}
\papersectiontarget{short249-base-six}
Consider the $43$ indexed sections through level two in base six.
The $37$ retained sections are $F_{0,0},F_{1,0}$, the five
$F_{1,r}$ ($1\le r\le5$), and the $30$ sections $F_{2,r}$ with
$1\le r<36$ and $6\nmid r$. The omitted sections satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_{2,0}&=6F_{1,0}, & F_{2,6}&=2F_{1,1}, & F_{2,12}&=4F_{1,2},\\
 F_{2,18}&=3F_{1,3}, & F_{2,24}&=4F_{1,4}, & F_{2,30}&=2F_{1,5}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus an integral combination of the $43$ sections vanishes precisely
when these six substitutions leave all $37$ retained coefficients zero.
If it vanishes, its six original omitted coefficients are its unique
coordinates in the relation basis of
Corollary~\ref{cor:integral-normal-form}.
At base two and level two the corresponding relation basis is simply
$E_{2,0}-2E_{1,0},\ E_{2,2}-E_{1,1}$.

We can also decide equality by finitely many evaluations. Order the
retained columns as above, with residues increasing within each level,
and evaluate at $n=0,\ldots,36$ to obtain a $37\times37$ matrix.
Its determinant is $17$ modulo $101$, so it is nonsingular over $\Q$
and detects every nonzero retained combination at one of these inputs.
The script \texttt{scripts/totient\_kernel\_normal\_form.py} calculates
the coordinates and the first such input using integer arithmetic.
The equality test uses the integer evaluations. Reducing the proposed
combination modulo $101$ would miss $101F_{2,2}$, for example,
although its value at $n=0$ is $101$.

\section{Bounded functions of totient residues}\label{sec:family}
\papersectiontarget{short249-residues}
Prime isolation also distinguishes rational from irrational values for
bounded functions of $\varphi(n)$ modulo a fixed integer. We use the
least nonnegative representative for a residue in an ordinary sum.
\begin{theorem}[Residue series and dyadic observables]\label{res:residueseries}
For every $m\ge3$,
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\ph(n)\bmod m}{2^n}\notin\Q.
\]
For $k\ge1$ and $f:\Z/2^k\Z\to\Q$, the series
$\sum_{n\ge1}f(\ph(n)\bmod2^k)2^{-n}$ is rational exactly when $f$ is
constant on the even residue classes. If that constant is $c$, its value is
$3f(1)/4+c/4$.
\end{theorem}
Modulo two the sum is $3/4$, since $\varphi(n)$ is even for $n\ge3$.
More generally only $f(1)$ and the even residue classes occur in the
second assertion. Its proof uses the following elementary tail argument.
\begin{lemma}[An isolated nonzero coefficient between long zero blocks]\label{lem:bounded-pulse}
Let $a_n\in\Z$ satisfy $|a_n|\le C$. Suppose that for arbitrarily large $L$
there is $N>L$ such that $a_N\ne0$ and
$a_{N+t}=0$ for $0<|t|\le L$. Then $\sum_{n\ge1}a_n2^{-n}$ is irrational.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that the sum has denominator $q\ge1$, so that every scaled
tail $T_j=\sum_{t\ge1}a_{j+t}2^{-t}$ belongs to $q^{-1}\Z$.
We choose an isolated coefficient with $C2^{-L}<1/q$.
The zero block to its right gives $|T_N|\le C2^{-L}<1/q$ and
therefore $T_N=0$, whereas the block to its left then gives
$T_{N-L-1}=a_N2^{-L-1}$, a nonzero element of $q^{-1}\Z$ of
absolute value less than $1/q$. This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}
For example, the indicator of the squares satisfies the lemma.
An eventually periodic sequence with infinitely many nonzero terms
does not have isolated coefficients at arbitrarily large distances.
The uniform bound $C$ is essential to this argument.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{res:residueseries}]
Fix a unit $s\pmod m$ and a positive integer $L$.
Choose distinct primes $q_t\equiv1\pmod m$, all exceeding $L$, for
$0<|t|\le L$. The congruences
\[
 p\equiv s\pmod m,\qquad p\equiv-t\pmod{q_t}\quad(0<|t|\le L)
\]
define a reduced progression, since $s$ is a unit modulo $m$ and
$0<|t|<q_t$. CRT and Dirichlet's theorem therefore allow us to choose
an arbitrarily large prime $p>L+1$ on this progression, with
$m\mid\varphi(p+t)$ at every neighbour and
$\varphi(p)\equiv s-1\pmod m$ at the centre.

Subtract $f(0)$ and multiply by a common denominator of the values of
$f$. If $f(s-1)\ne f(0)$, the resulting bounded integer sequence has
an isolated nonzero coefficient for every $L$, so the lemma applies.
For the least-residue map we take $s=-1$ and obtain the nonzero
residue $m-2$ when $m\ge3$. When $m=2^k$, each even residue $r$
is of the form $s-1$ with $s$ a unit, so nonconstancy on the even
classes gives an irrational sum by the same argument.
Conversely, if $f$ is constant there, all terms after $n=2$ equal
$c$ and we obtain $3f(1)/4+c/4$.
\end{proof}

The first assertion is also an application of Erd\H{o}s's sparse-series
criterion~\cite[Lem.~1, p.~213]{erdos1957}, and of Kaneko, Suzuki and
Tachiya~\cite[Thm.~3]{kst2026}. The nonzero residues occur on a set of
density zero: an integer in that set has no prime divisor
$p\equiv1\pmod m$. Wong gave the case in which the base equals the
modulus~\cite{wong2015}. The isolated-coefficient argument above also
treats signed values of $f-f(0)$, for which those nonnegative
sparse-series criteria need an additional spacing hypothesis.
The \longrecord{long249-r3-residue-extension}{recorded formal extension}
treats every integer base $t\ge2$ and proves rational linear
independence of $1$ and $\sum_n(\varphi(n)\bmod m)t^{-dn}$, $d\ge1$.

\section{The binary totient series}\label{sec:carry-rank}
\papersectiontarget{short249-tails}
For the unreduced totient, $0\le\varphi(n)\le n$ gives convergence of
$S$, but no fixed coefficient bound for Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-pulse}.
In fact, forcing $2^L\mid\varphi(N+L)$ with $N+L>1$ gives
$2^L\le N+L-1$. The resulting tail envelope
$(N+L+2)2^{-L}>1$ cannot exclude a positive rational denominator.
We instead consider differences of scaled tails.

Put $R_N=\sum_{j\ge1}\varphi(N+j)2^{-j}$ and
$\Delta_h(N)=R_{N+h}-R_N$. Subtracting the finite-prefix identities
for $2^{N+h}S$ and $2^NS$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-phase}
 \Delta_h(N)\equiv2^N(2^h-1)S\pmod\Z.
\end{equation}
If $S=a/(2^cv)$, with $v$ odd, then $\Delta_h(N)$ is integral for
$N\ge c$ and $h=\varphi(v)$. To disprove rationality it therefore
suffices to find a nonintegral difference beyond every threshold for
each positive $h$.

For $h,N,L\in\N$, define the integer
\[
 D_{h,N,L}=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
   \bigl(\varphi(N+h+1+j)-\varphi(N+1+j)\bigr)2^{L-1-j},
 \qquad B=N+h+L+2.
\]
Since $0\le R_M\le M+2$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-error}
 2^L\Delta_h(N)-D_{h,N,L}=R_{N+h+L}-R_{N+L},\qquad
 |2^L\Delta_h(N)-D_{h,N,L}|\le B.
\end{equation}
The difference is bounded by the larger tail bound. Hence the finite
inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r3-certificate}
 B<D_{h,N,L}\bmod2^L<2^L-B
\end{equation}
forces $\Delta_h(N)\notin\Z$.
Conversely, if its distance to $\Z$ is positive, choose $L$ so that
$2^L\|\Delta_h(N)\|_{\R/\Z}>2B$; the same error estimate gives
\eqref{eq:r3-certificate}. Thus allowing an unrestricted depth loses
no nonintegral difference.
For example, $D_{1,12,16}=-143140$ has residue $53468$ modulo $2^{16}$,
and $B=31$, so it excludes denominators dividing $2^{12}$.

\subsection{Equivalent residue conditions}\label{sec:frontier}
We write $K(h,N,L)$ for \eqref{eq:r3-certificate}.
\begin{theorem}[Propagation of one nonintegral tail difference]\label{res:fulldepth}
Fix $d\ge1$ and $N\ge0$. If $\Delta_d(N)\notin\Z$, then every sufficiently
late pair $\{t,t+1\}$ contains an $m$ such that $K(md,N,md)$ holds.
Consequently
\[
 \exists t\ge1:\ K(td,N,td)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Delta_d(N)\notin\Z.
\]
Requiring this for every $d\ge1,N\ge0$ is equivalent to $S\notin\Q$.
\end{theorem}
The proof uses the recurrence
$\Delta_{(t+1)d}(N)\equiv2^d\Delta_{td}(N)+\Delta_d(N)\pmod\Z$;
it is given in \longrecord{long249-r3-ray}{Appendix A of the long record}.
A single pair $d,N$ need not imply irrationality; the final assertion
requires every positive $d$ and every $N\ge0$.

There is a second equivalent test for each hypothetical denominator
$2^cv$. For a positive multiple $H$ of $\varphi(v)$, set
$M=(2^H-1)/v$ and
$B_{H,c}=\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}\varphi(c+1+j)2^{H-1-j}$.
\begin{equivform}[A residue test modulo a divisor of $2^H-1$]\label{res:canonicalmersenne}
The series $S$ is irrational if and only if, for every $c\ge0$ and odd
$v\ge1$, some positive multiple $H$ of $\ph(v)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-gap}
 c+H+1<(-B_{H,c})\bmod M<M-(c+H+1).
\end{equation}
\end{equivform}
The \longrecord{long249-r3-mersenne}{proof and residue recurrence} use
$B_{H,c}=M(vR_c)-\Delta_H(c)$ and the linear tail bound.
Neither test provides these
universal witnesses. A separate
\longrecord{long249-r3-farey}{Farey-interval calculation} at depth $240$
excludes $S=a/q$ for $0<q\le Q_0\approx7.96\times10^{34}$.

\subsection{Comparison sequences}\label{sec:nogo}\label{sec:signed-interpolation-transfer}
Large ranks alone cannot settle the series question. There is an integer
sequence $c$ with $0\le c(n)\le n$, unchanged odd totients and
$|c(n)-\varphi(n)|\le2$ at even indices, for which
$\sum c(n)2^{-n}=5/4$. Its integral carry still has dyadic rank at least
$2^e-1$ through every positive level.
The \longrecord{long249-r3-comparisons}{construction and its limitations}
explain why it fails the exact prime-dilation laws of $\varphi$.
The same section retains the cofinal LCM-diagonal criterion, its endpoint
sign conditions and the limits of finite integer combinations of shifts.

A more flexible \longrecord{long249-r3-interpolation}{signed interpolation theorem}
fills an interval about $\sum\varphi(n)\beta^{-n}$, for each integer
$\beta\ge2$, while preserving a prescribed prefix, all odd coefficients
and errors bounded by any positive $f(n)\to\infty$.
Its even support has upper Banach density zero. Every fixed progression
has an entire Dirichlet defect. At each root of unity the generating-function
defect has smooth radial data, with the prescribed finite jets zero. The theorem is an ordinary proof;
it preserves Dirichlet singularities, not regular parts or zeros, and
finite jets do not specify an entire radial germ. In particular it
does not prove cancellation for the unchanged totient.

Rational approximation imposes a different requirement. The identity
\[
 \Theta_2:=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{\mu(d)}{(2^d-1)^2}=S-\frac12
\]
was posted by Amiram Eldar, together with its coprimality interpretation,
on 15 March 2026~\cite{eldar2026oeis}, and by Steve Fan on
16 May 2026~\cite{fan2026totient}.
Consider the rational quotients
\[
 Q(e,Y)=\frac{\bigl(\sum_{d\le Y}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)^{e+2}\bigr)^2}
      {\sum_{d\le Y}\mu(d)/(2^d-1)^{2e+2}}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[A lower bound for the rank-one quotients]\label{res:rankonefloor}
For $e\ge1$ and $Y\ge4$, the denominator of $Q(e,Y)$ is positive, and the
unique minimiser is $(e,Y)=(1,5)$. Every admissible quotient and every
nonempty finite positive weighted average of such quotients satisfies
\[
 Q-\Theta_2>\frac{21}{320}.
\]
The uniform bound $Q-\Theta_2>1/15$ is false already at $(1,5)$.
\end{theorem}
The \longrecord{long249-r3-rankone}{proof in the long record}
bounds the infinite tails geometrically and compares seven-term
rational prefixes.
This concerns averages taken after forming the quotients;
signed combinations, coupling before division and higher-rank kernels
are separate questions. A family of rational approximants $p_j/q_j$
in lowest terms would instead need
$0<q_j|\Theta_2-p_j/q_j|\to0$. Mere convergence does not give it.

\section{An exponential-sum question}\label{sec:open}
\papersectiontarget{short249-first-harmonic}
For every $h\ge1$ and every $X_0$, suppose that some integers
$X\ge\max(X_0,1)$ and $L\ge h$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r3-harmonic}
 16(2X+h+L+2)\le2^L,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{N=X}^{2X-1}
       \exp(2\pi iD_{h,N,L}/2^L)\le\frac9{10}X.
\end{equation}
These inequalities would prove irrationality. Indeed, if every residue
failed \eqref{eq:r3-certificate}, each phase would be within $1/16$
of an integer, so every real part would be at least
$\cos(\pi/8)>9/10$. A certificate must therefore occur on the block,
and \eqref{eq:tail-phase} excludes every rational denominator by
choosing its period and then a sufficiently late block.

The following decomposition isolates the estimates still needed.
Fix $h,s\ge1$, $L\ge s+h$, $X\ge1$ and $0<\eta<1$; put
$t=L-s+1$ and $E_N=\exp(2\pi iD_{h,N,L}/2^L)$.
For $X\le N<2X$, let $p_N$ be the largest prime factor of $N+t$ and
$m_N=(N+t)/p_N$. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A&=\{N\in[X,2X):m_N\le\lfloor\sqrt X/2\rfloor,
                      \ p_N>2\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\},\\
 \mathcal G&=\{N\in\mathcal A:\varphi(m_N)\ge\eta m_N\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All indices are integers. On $\mathcal A$ we have $p_N>m_N$, so
$\varphi(N+t)=\varphi(m_N)(p_N-1)$. Its coefficient in the phase is
$(2^h-1)/2^t$. Write
\[
 z_N=\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i(2^h-1)\varphi(m_N)(p_N-1)}{2^t}\right),
 \qquad w_N=E_N/z_N,
\]
and let $\bar z_m$ be the average over the indices in $\mathcal A$
with cofactor $m$, with value zero for an empty set. The four proposed
bounds are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r3-four-bounds}
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Re}\sum_{N\in\mathcal G}w_N(z_N-\bar z_{m_N})
       &\le\tfrac{14}{25}X,\\
 \left|\sum_{N\in\mathcal G}w_N\bar z_{m_N}\right|
       &\le\tfrac1{100}X,\\
 \left|\sum_{N\in\mathcal A\smallsetminus\mathcal G}E_N\right|
       &\le\tfrac1{100}X,\\
 \left|\sum_{\substack{X\le N<2X\\N\notin\mathcal A}}E_N\right|
       &\le\tfrac8{25}X.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Before taking real parts and absolute values, the four sums add to
$\sum E_N$, and hence their bounds imply the real-part estimate
$9X/10$ in \eqref{eq:r3-harmonic}. We have only added and subtracted
the group means, without any independence assumption. The resulting
bound concerns the real part, and does not give a $21X/25$ bound
for the absolute value of the full sum.

\begin{problem}[Four estimates for the first-harmonic sum]
\label{prob:firstharmonic}
For each $h\ge1$, do there exist $s\ge1$ and $0<\eta<1$ such that, for every
$X_0$, there are $X,L$ with
\[
 \max(X_0,1)\le X,\qquad h\le L-s,\qquad
 16(2X+h+L+2)\le2^L,
\]
for which those four bounds hold?
\end{problem}

All four estimates must hold on the same block with the same parameters.
For each fixed $h,s$, take the least admissible $L$ satisfying the room
inequality and $L\ge s+h$. The
\longrecord{long249-r3-counts}{smooth-number and small-cofactor estimates}
give the last two bounds for all sufficiently large $X$ when
$0<\eta<1/(300\log4)$. The elementary excluded-cofactor estimate is
$(3\eta\log4+o(1))X$; it needs no prime number theorem.
The group-mean and weighted mean-subtracted estimates remain open.
No optimality is asserted for the sufficient margins in
\eqref{eq:r3-four-bounds}.

Alternatively, at $s=26$, $\eta=1/1000$ and minimal depth one has
$|\mathcal G|>67X/100$ for large $X$. The single open estimate
$\operatorname{Re}\sum_{N\in\mathcal G}E_N\le603X/1000$, for each
$h$ on arbitrarily large blocks, would suffice by the same argument.
The \longrecord{long249-r3-interpolation}{rational comparison sequences}
force larger first harmonics at all sufficiently large admissible scales;
this describes their difference from the required behaviour of $\varphi$.

Balasubramanian, Giri and Srivastav give correlation estimates applicable
to $\varphi(n)/n$, uniformly for $|h|\le x/2$~\cite[Thm.~2.2]{bgs2016}.
Here the modulus grows with $L$, and the residual weight depends on
other totient values in the window. That correlation theorem supplies
neither of the two remaining estimates. Establishing them for the
actual totient is the unresolved arithmetic step.

\appendix
\section{Regularity and formal sources}\label{app:sources}
\papersectiontarget{short249-sources}
Coons's proof of nonregularity counts the poles of
$\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)$~\cite[Cor.~3.1, Thm.~3.2]{coons}.
For an integer-valued sequence, finite dimension of the rational kernel
span is equivalent to finite generation of its integral kernel module:
choose finitely many evaluations that embed the span in $\Q^d$;
the module embeds in $\Z^d$, whose subgroups are finitely generated
(compare~\cite[App.~A]{coons-jsr}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:kkernelrank} recovers this nonregularity and gives
the ranks of its finite truncations.
For an account of the earlier separation method, see Allouche, Shallit
and Yassawi~\cite[Thm.~3, Ex.~4, Rem.~7]{allouche-shallit-yassawi}.

Martin's theorem also proves the affine independence directly. Put a
nonzero coefficient first in a proposed relation and choose the
successive totient ratios larger than
$\max(1,\sum_{i>1}|c_i|/|c_1|)$. On the positive-lower-density set
provided by his theorem, the first term then dominates the sum of the
others in absolute value~\cite[Thm.~1]{martin-phi-inequalities}.

These facts have a functional consequence. A $k$-Mahler equation has
the form $\sum_{i=0}^d P_i(z)F(z^{k^i})=0$, with
$P_i\in\Q[z]$ and $P_0\ne0$.
The height theorem of Adamczewski, Bell and Smertnig implies that a
$k$-Mahler series with integer coefficients of polynomial growth is
$k$-regular~\cite[Thm.~1.2(b)]{adamczewski-bell-smertnig2023}.
Since $\varphi(n)\le n$, its logarithmic height is at most $\log n$,
so $F_\varphi(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(n)z^n$ has no such equation.
Bell and Smertnig's multiplicative classification gives the same
conclusion~\cite[Thm.~1.3]{bell-smertnig2026}.
Bell, Bruin and Coons treat related algebraic and $D$-finite series,
the latter satisfying a linear differential equation with polynomial
coefficients~\cite[Thms.~1.5--1.6]{bell-bruin-coons}.
These statements concern the function, and do not determine
$F_\varphi(1/2)$.

The section operators $T_i g(n)=g(kn+i)$ satisfy
\[
 T_iF_{j,r}=F_{j+1,k^ji+r},\qquad
 V_{k,e+1}=V_{k,e}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}T_iV_{k,e}.
\]
To obtain the second identity, write every residue at level $j+1$
as $k^ji+r$. For a positive retained residue, the resulting section
is again retained. For the two zero-residue generators, use
$T_iF_{0,0}=F_{1,i}$ and $T_iF_{1,0}=F_{2,ki}$ followed by the
zero-residue or scalar reduction. We thus obtain a positive integer
multiple of a basis element in every case, although the increasing
dimensions prevent any finite truncation from being invariant.
The growth formula using the joint spectral radius for finite-dimensional regular
sequences~\cite[Thm.~1, Prop.~4, Cor.~7]{coons-jsr} therefore does not
apply to $\varphi$.
